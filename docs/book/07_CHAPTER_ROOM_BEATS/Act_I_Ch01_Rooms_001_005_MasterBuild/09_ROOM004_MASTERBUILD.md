# Room 004 — MasterBuild Blueprint (10×)
**Chapter 01 / Rooms 001–005**  
**Room Function:** INTERNAL PRESSURE (Turn Inward)  
**Act:** I (Unease → Personal Discomfort)

This file defines the complete structural, symbolic, emotional, and linguistic blueprint for Room 004.
No prose is written until this blueprint is locked.

---

## A. Prime Directive

Room 004 turns the tension **inward** without accusation.

Target internal reader response:
> “Why am *I* paying attention to this?”

Not:
- “This place is dangerous.”
- “Someone is doing this to me.”
- “I’ve been tricked.”

The house does not apply pressure.  
The reader **feels** it.

---

## B. Position in the 5-Room Circuit

**Incoming state (Room 3):**
- Mild unease
- Unnamed fracture
- Reality slightly unreliable

**Outgoing state (Room 5):**
- Personal discomfort
- Quiet self-awareness
- Emotional residue without conclusion

The locus of tension shifts from the environment to the reader’s interior.

---

## C. Emotional & Nervous System Profile

- Emotion: personal discomfort, self-consciousness
- Nervous system: light sympathetic engagement with quick return to baseline
- Success signal: the reader notices their own noticing

Room 004 should feel *close*, not threatening.

---

## D. Mechanism of Pressure (Choose One)

Room 004 may introduce **exactly one** inward pressure mechanism.

Allowed mechanisms (choose one later, not now):
- **Attention pressure:** the reader becomes aware of where they are looking or listening
- **Choice pressure:** a benign choice feels oddly consequential
- **Expectation pressure:** the reader anticipates something that does not happen
- **Timing pressure:** a pause lasts a fraction longer than expected

Do not stack mechanisms.

---

## E. Symbol Set (Human-Scale Only)

Symbols in Room 004 must be **non-institutional** and human-scale.

Allowed:
- Body cues (breath, posture, hesitation)
- Small domestic decisions (where to stand, whether to touch something)
- Threshold moments (half-steps, pauses, hovering)

Disallowed:
- Written rules or instructions
- Surveillance cues
- Authority markers
- Mechanical systems

The pressure should feel self-generated.

---

## F. Language & Diction Rules

Allowed:
- First hints of interiority (without analysis)
- Sensory-emotional crossover (“it feels quieter than before”)
- Conditional phrasing

Required technique:
- **Proximity language** (close, near, just, here)

Banned:
- blame
- guilt
- compliance
- expectation-setting (“you should,” “you are meant to”)

The reader must never feel judged.

---

## G. Rhythm & Pacing Design

- Slightly tighter than Room 3
- Fewer descriptive flourishes
- One sentence that folds inward (reflexive, but not analytical)

Paragraph rule:
- Paragraphs shorten toward the end of the room

This creates gentle compression.

---

## H. Micro-Beat Spine

1. Continuation with awareness
2. Subtle inward shift (attention turns to self)
3. A moment of hesitation or pause
4. Pressure without cause
5. Movement forward without relief

The reader continues not because they are pushed, but because stopping feels awkward.

---

## I. What This Room Must NOT Do

Room 004 must NOT:
- Assign responsibility
- Explain the pressure
- Escalate threat
- Introduce systems or intent
- Offer reassurance or release

Relief belongs to later Acts.

---

## J. Future Payoff Awareness

Room 004 seeds:
- the habit of self-monitoring
- normalized internal compliance
- the feeling of “adjusting oneself” to a space

Later Acts will reinterpret this as learned behavior.
Do not name it here.

---

## K. Acceptance Gates

A Room 004 prose draft is approved only if:

1. Pressure is internal, not environmental
2. No accusation language appears
3. Exactly one pressure mechanism exists
4. The reader still feels free to stop
5. Room 5 feels emotionally inevitable

---

## Editorial Warning

Room 004 often fails by becoming psychological exposition.

If it explains feelings, it’s wrong.  
If it comforts the reader, it’s premature.  
If it frightens the reader, it’s too much.

Correct tone:
> “I’m not sure why that mattered.”
