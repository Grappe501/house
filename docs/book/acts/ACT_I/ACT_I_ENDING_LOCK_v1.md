# ACT_I_ENDING_LOCK_v1
**Ending Architecture for Act I (Rooms 21–25)**  
_Status: Structural Lock_

This document defines the **load-bearing literary blueprint** for Rooms **21–25** (end of Act I).
It is **not prose**. It is the armature every future draft, audio file, and HTML page must obey.

Once accepted, these rooms are **structurally immutable**.

---

## Global design constraints (apply to all five rooms)

- **No villains**
- **No threats**
- **No calls to action**
- **No overt political language**
- **No emotional spikes**

- **Calm language only**
- **Neutral tone**
- **Familiar metaphors**
- **Reader autonomy appears intact**
- **Discomfort arises after reading, not during**

**Primary emotional goal:** *Delayed recognition*

---

## Cross-room reader arc (mandatory)

| Room | Reader state on entry | Reader state on exit |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | Curious, alert | Relaxed, trusting |
| 22 | Comfortable | Slightly dulled |
| 23 | Neutral | Validated, faintly hollow |
| 24 | Receptive | Uneasy, guided |
| 25 | Open | Clear, implicated |

**No room may resolve tension.**  
Each room may only **reposition** it.

---

## Room 21 — “The Well-Kept Place”

### Non-negotiable function
Normalize the system by presenting it as competent, orderly, and benign.

### Emotional design
- **Entry:** mild relief  
- **Exit:** trust  
The reader must *exhale* here.

### Symbolic elements
- clean surfaces, straight lines  
- even lighting  
- absence of clutter  

**Meaning:** order = virtue; stability = goodness

### Language rules
**Allowed:** “maintained”, “reliable”, “working as intended”, “taken care of”  
**Forbidden:** “power”, “control”, “choice”, “freedom”

### Audio / silence
Low ambient hum **or** silence. No melody. No emotional cueing.

### Door logic
Door visible immediately; opens without effort; no alternatives presented.

### Exit condition
Reader believes: “This place knows what it’s doing.”

### Failure mode (do not do)
Do not introduce mystery, manipulation hints, or danger foreshadowing.  
This room must feel **earned**, not suspicious.

---

## Room 22 — “The Routine”

### Non-negotiable function
Introduce repetition as comfort, not constraint.

### Emotional design
- **Entry:** settled  
- **Exit:** slightly dulled  
Reader should feel time passing without noticing.

### Symbolic elements
- repeated actions  
- cyclical descriptions  
- familiar phrasing reused intentionally  

**Meaning:** habit replaces decision.

### Language rules
**Allowed:** “again”, “as usual”, “the same as before”, “nothing unexpected”  
**Forbidden:** “stuck”, “loop”, “cycle”, “trap”

### Audio / silence
Repetition if audio exists; otherwise silence.

### Door logic
Door appears *after* routine completes; placement feels logical, not dramatic.

### Exit condition
Reader thinks: “I’ve done this before.”

### Failure mode
No disruption, no escalation, no introduced choice.

---

## Room 23 — “The Thank You”

### Non-negotiable function
Reveal how compliance is emotionally rewarded.

### Emotional design
- **Entry:** neutral  
- **Exit:** validated, slightly empty  
This is the most dangerous room emotionally.

### Symbolic elements
- praise without specifics  
- gratitude without outcome  
- appreciation detached from power  

**Meaning:** belonging as currency.

### Language rules
**Allowed:** “people like you”, “it matters”, “you’re needed”, “it wouldn’t work without you”  
**Forbidden:** explaining what is being supported; measurable results.

### Audio / silence
Warm tone if audio exists; no climax; no resolution.

### Door logic
Door appears as a courtesy; opens gently.

### Exit condition
Reader feels: “I matter here.” and faintly: “But to what?”

### Failure mode
Do not overplay warmth, tip into sentimentality, or expose the mechanism.

---

## Room 24 — “The Easy Door”

### Non-negotiable function
Introduce **conditional freedom** without force.

### Emotional design
- **Entry:** open  
- **Exit:** uneasy  
First conscious discomfort.

### Symbolic elements
- one clear path  
- other paths vaguely referenced  
- ease contrasted with ambiguity  

**Meaning:** freedom shaped by friction.

### Language rules
**Allowed:** “simple”, “efficient”, “straightforward”, “most people choose”  
**Forbidden:** “blocked”, “closed”, “not allowed”

### Audio / silence
Neutral; slight narrowing of soundscape if used.

### Door logic
One door obvious; others exist only conceptually.

### Exit condition
Reader realizes: “I wasn’t forced. I was guided.”

### Failure mode
No coercion implication, no moralizing, no “heroic resistance” framing.

---

## Room 25 — “The House Speaks” (Act I terminus — immutable)

### Non-negotiable function
Reframe the house as a system that **requires the reader**.

### Emotional design
- **Entry:** alert  
- **Exit:** clear, implicated  
No fear. No anger. Only clarity.

### Symbolic elements
- language shifts from *space* to *function*  
- house speaks neutrally  
- no apology, no menace  

**Meaning:** the system does not hate you; it needs you.

### Language rules
**Allowed:** “the house works”, “it always has”, “you can stay”, “most people do”  
**Forbidden:** “if you don’t”, “or else”, any threat framing.

### Audio / silence
Silence preferred. If audio exists, it ends abruptly.

### Door logic
No clear exit presented; no next step explained.

### Final line (locked)
> “You can stay.  
> Most people do.  
> The house needs you.”

### Exit condition
Reader knows:
1. this is a system  
2. they participate in it  
3. participation has been framed as freedom

### Failure mode (critical)
Do not escalate, explain, or soften. Nothing should feel resolved.

## Handoff spec — Room 26 (for future use)

Room 26 must assume:
- reader is awake but not activated  
- trust is cracked, not broken  
- curiosity is now personal  

Room 26 must not comfort; it must orient.
