# Act I — Rooms 16–20 Plan

**Module:** The Maze → The Clock → The Counter → The Witness → The Beam

## Module intent
This module returns to compression after the breath of Rooms 11–15. The House increases friction through procedural loops, time theft, and invisible accounting. The narrator is pushed toward exhaustion and self-doubt—then given a Witness moment that proves the pattern is real. Room 20 is the Beam: a clean, earned clarity that prevents collapse and sets up the Act I door (Room 25).

## Reader movement
- Rules shift and loop (learned helplessness revealed as design).
- Time is stolen through micro-delays (exhaustion is structural).
- Accounting appears (debts/credits assigned without consent).
- A witness artifact proves the pattern (gaslighting breaks).
- The Beam installs usable language + steadiness for the next cycle.

## Rooms 16–20 breakdown
### Room 16: The Maze
- **Subtitle:** You can do everything right and still not arrive.
- **Psychology target:** Induce learned helplessness via shifting rules. Show friction as design, not accident.
- **Emotional move:** Motivation → confusion → frustration → numbness.
- **Symbols:**
  - **Corridor with identical doors** — choices that aren’t choices
  - **Signage that contradicts itself** — rules designed to fail you
  - **Numbered tickets** — your time converted into waiting
  - **A map that doesn’t match** — orientation removed
  - **Soft carpet that swallows footsteps** — dissent muffled
- **Prose beats:**
  - Narrator enters a corridor system that feels administrative and endless.
  - Directions are offered politely, then conflict.
  - The narrator repeats a loop and notices the loop (first proof of maze).
  - A small humiliation: being 'late' to a place they couldn’t reach.
  - Exit: narrator chooses not the 'right' door but the next door—agency reduced to survival.
- **Handoff:** The loop steals time; the narrator realizes the House keeps time like a weapon.

### Room 17: The Clock
- **Subtitle:** Time theft without a thief.
- **Psychology target:** Make exhaustion structural: time drains through micro-delays, resets, and invisible requirements.
- **Emotional move:** Urgency → fatigue → resignation → quiet anger.
- **Symbols:**
  - **Clock that runs slightly fast** — permanent insufficiency
  - **Chime that interrupts thought** — attention controlled
  - **Checklist that repopulates** — work that cannot be finished
  - **A chair that’s almost comfortable** — waiting as normalization
  - **Dimmer that follows the clock** — mood tethered to schedule
- **Prose beats:**
  - Narrator notices the clock is off, but always against them.
  - Interruptions arrive at the moment focus forms.
  - The narrator completes tasks that reappear with new wording.
  - Fatigue becomes the story; anger stays contained and accurate.
  - Exit: narrator follows the sound of counting—someone is keeping score.
- **Handoff:** A faint count leads toward the counting room: the House’s ledger.

### Room 18: The Counter
- **Subtitle:** Everything is counted; nothing is explained.
- **Psychology target:** Reveal invisible accounting: costs, debts, credits—assigned without consent.
- **Emotional move:** Alarm → disbelief → dread → clarity.
- **Symbols:**
  - **Ledger with columns** — moral math and debt
  - **Red ink entries** — punishment disguised as accounting
  - **A stamp: 'INSUFFICIENT'** — never enough
  - **Receipt for 'care'** — love turned into bill
  - **Calculator with missing keys** — you cannot audit the system
- **Prose beats:**
  - Narrator finds a ledger tracking behaviors as debits/credits.
  - Entries use virtue language: 'cooperation', 'tone', 'gratitude'.
  - Charges appear for things like 'disruption' and 'asking twice'.
  - Narrator tries to compute totals but the tools won’t allow it.
  - Exit: narrator realizes they need a witness—proof outside their own mind.
- **Handoff:** The narrator seeks a witness: someone or something that can confirm the pattern.

### Room 19: The Witness
- **Subtitle:** Evidence that isn’t memory.
- **Psychology target:** Validate reality. Provide proof that breaks gaslighting and restores trust in perception.
- **Emotional move:** Suspicion → discovery → relief → resolve.
- **Symbols:**
  - **Duplicate document** — proof outside feeling
  - **Two mismatched timestamps** — time manipulation
  - **A recording / transcript fragment** — the House can’t erase everything
  - **A hidden note from another person** — you are not alone
  - **A smudge that doesn’t reset** — the reset can fail
- **Prose beats:**
  - Narrator finds an artifact the House failed to clean away.
  - A second copy contradicts the official record.
  - The narrator realizes the House edits—therefore it fears truth.
  - Relief is immediate but controlled: breath without celebration.
  - Exit: narrator carries the proof toward the Beam room—clarity must be spoken plainly.
- **Handoff:** With proof in hand, the narrator is ready for the Beam: a statement that is true and usable.

### Room 20: Beam Room
- **Subtitle:** A clean truth you can carry.
- **Psychology target:** Install an earned thesis fragment: it’s not personal; it’s patterned. Give the reader usable language.
- **Emotional move:** Stillness → clarity → strength → forward motion.
- **Symbols:**
  - **Straight line / plumb line** — truth as alignment
  - **Window or skylight** — light without permission
  - **A simple sentence on a card** — language as tool, not trap
  - **The hum becoming background** — the system loses primacy
  - **A door labeled 'Next' (no flourish)** — progress without performance
- **Prose beats:**
  - The room is spare; it feels like relief and honesty.
  - Narrator lays out the proof without dramatizing it.
  - A single sentence lands: the pattern is designed; your body was right.
  - The narrator feels steadier—less reactive, more oriented.
  - Exit: narrator turns back into the House with agency intact.
- **Handoff:** Room 21 can tighten again—now the reader has language and proof, not just feelings.
