# Act IV — Maintenance (INTEGRATE)
_Last updated: 2026-02-03_

Act IV is the landing gear and the runway.

Act I built the house.  
Act II taught the reader to see systems without panic.  
Act III rebuilt cognition: pressure, cost, drift, durability.

**Act IV turns all of that into lived practice.**  
Not a speech. Not a mood. Not a finale. A **culture of maintenance**.

---

## The mission of the last act
By Room 100, the reader should feel:

1. **Belonging without submission** — “This is ours” without waiting for permission.
2. **Agency without aggression** — action that doesn’t require an enemy to exist.
3. **Competence without jargon** — tools and practices that feel like normal life.
4. **Durability without heroics** — stability as feedback, schedules, and shared memory.
5. **Proof as care** — the reader trusts because the system shows its work.

The act’s emotional crescendo is not hype.
It is **quiet capability** that becomes irreversible.

---

## Non-negotiables (the guardrails)
- No triumphal ending. No “we won.” No “final battle.”
- No villain-focus. Systems, patterns, incentives — not enemies.
- No coercion. No “you must.” (Invitation is allowed only as orientation to tools.)
- No jargon without translation. If a concept is necessary, it becomes household language.
- Maintenance is governance: the story treats “keeping things working” as power.

---

## Symbol spine (thick metaphor, consistent physics)
**House / Threshold:** permission and belonging; who feels allowed inside.  
**Porch:** human-scale legitimacy; trust loops; presence.  
**Thermostat / Cadence:** stability through feedback and rhythm.  
**Level on the shelf:** drift detection; humility; noticing lean early.  
**Flashlight under floorboards:** loopholes; shadows created by rules.  
**Ledger / Repair log:** legibility; memory; non-performative accountability.  
**Open door:** trust dividend; compounding returns.  
**Shared manual:** knowledge as public infrastructure; anti-capture immunity.  
**Lab:** proof layer; “show your work”; reality-testing as care.

These symbols must recur in small ways so that, by the time the book names
civic mechanics explicitly, the reader feels the concepts as already familiar.

---

## The act arc (how the crescendo works)
Act IV is five 5-room chapters.
Each chapter stands alone as a practice module.
Together, they stack into a life trajectory shift.

### Chapter 16 (076–080) — Permission → Presence
The reader learns: agency starts with **consent and contact**, not conflict.
**Beam at 079:** drift detection is the beginning of legitimacy.

### Chapter 17 (081–085) — Porch Method → New Default
The reader learns: small asks become systems; repetition builds trust.
**Beam at 084:** culture locks in; extraordinary becomes normal.

### Chapter 18 (086–090) — Tools Without Jargon → Anti-Capture Hygiene
The reader learns: participation is self-defense; rules need inspection.
**Beam at 087:** loophole hunting prevents silent erosion.

### Chapter 19 (091–095) — Building Together → Trust Dividend
The reader learns: maintenance is shared labor, logged and visible.
**Beam at 094:** trust is an output that compounds.

### Chapter 20 (096–100) — Epilogue → The Lab Door
The reader learns: the house survives turnover through public knowledge.
**Beam at 098:** nothing essential is secret.
The act ends with an invitation to proof and tools: **walk into the lab**.

## Wordcraft principles (every word matters)
- Prefer **nouns and verbs** that carry physics: *load, joint, cadence, ledger, drift, repair*.
- Avoid moral adjectives. Replace “good/bad” with “durable/fragile,” “legible/opaque,” “costly/affordable.”
- End paragraphs on **structural truths**, not emotional peaks.
- Repetition is allowed only when it functions like a refrain (porch / level / ledger / manual).

---

## What the reader should do (without being told to)
Act IV’s job is to make the next phase feel like a *choice the reader already owns*:
- They notice drift early.
- They choose human-scale trust loops.
- They keep records openly.
- They schedule review cycles.
- They prefer proof over charisma.

If we land this correctly, action in the next layer is not “activism.”
It is **maintenance** — the normal work of keeping a shared home standing.
