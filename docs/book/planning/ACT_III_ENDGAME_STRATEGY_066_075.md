# Act III Endgame — Strategic Finish (Rooms 066–075)
## “Frontways / Backways / Sideways / Another Dimension” Build Plan

This file is a drafting compass for the final two chapters of Act III.
It is written to ensure **every word carries weight** and the act lands cleanly into Act IV’s action phase without triggering panic or propaganda.

---

## 1) Frontways (forward motion)
**What the reader experiences:**
- 066–069: *the build becomes practical* (sequence, constraints, materials, interfaces)
- 070: *the joint sets* (no early loading)
- 071–074: *the new slope appears* (stress drops → QOL rises; stability holds; comparisons clarify)
- 075: *exit with a map, not an order*

**Key forward rule:**  
Act III ends with **capacity**, not command.

---

## 2) Backways (reverse engineering from Act IV)
Act IV is the “do-phase.”
To make Act IV feel ethical and achievable, Act III must deliver:
- A **yardstick** (QOL / cost-to-live) to prevent ideological drift
- A **thermostat frame** to prevent overheating/overreach
- A **sequencing frame** to prevent “everything at once” fantasy
- A **handoff frame** so effort doesn’t evaporate at interfaces
- A **quiet threshold** so action feels chosen, not compelled

**Reverse check:**  
If Act IV needs urgency, Act III should supply *clarity* so urgency doesn’t become coercion.

---

## 3) Sideways (parallel meanings)
Each room should operate on 3 parallel tracks:
1. **House track:** carpentry, joints, load, temperature, slope
2. **Mind track:** attention, fatigue, discernment, restraint
3. **System track:** sequencing, constraints, interfaces, feedback loops

**Sideways rule:**  
If a line only serves one track, it must justify its existence.

---

## 4) Another dimension (subtext & implication)
Act III should imply, without stating:
- Some systems can be rebuilt
- Some cannot
- Durability requires boring design
- Better futures feel less dramatic, not more

**Subtext rule:**  
Avoid “truth claims.” Prefer “build tests.”

---

## 5) The 10-room tension curve (micro-pacing)
- 066: tighten (practical focus)
- 067: tighten (physics/constraints)
- 068: widen (materials / realism)
- 069: tighten (interfaces are delicate)
- 070: release (cure)
- 071: widen (new slope)
- 072: tighten (measurement)
- 073: widen (stable normal)
- 074: tighten (compare clearly)
- 075: release (threshold)

---

## 6) Wordcraft constraints (“every word matters”)
- Prefer **short nouns + verbs** over adjectives
- Keep metaphors consistent: build/temperature/ledger/slope
- No flourish that can be read as recruitment
- No moralizing
- End paragraphs on **structural claims**, not emotional crescendos

---

## 7) Room-level drift tripwires
If any room includes:
- “we must / you must / it’s time / now is the moment” → RED
- “they / enemies / capture by them” → RED (use “structures”)
- “justice / accountability / truth” → YELLOW→ likely rewrite
- “hope / despair” language spikes → YELLOW

---

## 8) Deliverables checklist (what we will write next)
For each room 066–075, the prose draft must include:
- One **load-bearing sentence** (the room’s thesis)
- One **constraint sentence** (what this room refuses to promise)
- One **bridge sentence** (handoff to next room)
- One **quiet release** (lower heat before leaving)

---

## 9) How the two 5-room arcs stand alone
### Chapter 14 stands alone as:
A practical guide to transition thinking: sequence → constraints → materials → interfaces → cure.

### Chapter 15 stands alone as:
A proof-of-feel: trajectory → QOL ledger → thermostat → side-by-sides → threshold.

---

## 10) The final landing line (principle)
Act III ends when the reader can say:
“I know how to judge a build. I’m not being pushed. I’m ready to choose.”
