Working Majority Republic

Universal Utilities & Public Safety Infrastructure

Home / The Model / Infrastructure
Principle

Modern life depends on shared infrastructure

Water, power, broadband, roads, and emergency response are not luxury services—they are the operating system of society. A working-majority republic treats core infrastructure as a public utility layer with universal access, transparent pricing, and local accountability.

Goal: make public systems so reliable and fair that most people choose them by default.
Water utilities

Safe water is non-negotiable

  • Universal safe water standard: enforceable testing and transparency everywhere.
  • Replacement and modernization: lead service line replacement and aging pipe renewal prioritized.
  • Affordability protections: shutoff limits for hardship; payment plans that do not trap people.
  • Watershed integration: ties directly to the Environment pillar (polluter pays).
Fire and emergency services

Public safety is local and professional

  • Baseline service guarantees for response time and coverage by geography.
  • Mutual aid modernization across counties and states with shared training standards.
  • Volunteer support: rural volunteer departments receive equipment, training, and stipends to prevent collapse.
  • Resilience hubs: schools and community centers double as emergency support sites.
Outcome: fewer deaths in disasters; faster recovery; lower insurance and economic loss.
Universal broadband

Equal internet access is equal opportunity

  • Universal high-speed access as an infrastructure right for all households.
  • Open-access fiber where possible: multiple providers can compete on the same physical network.
  • Affordability tiers: a basic plan is guaranteed; higher tiers are optional.
  • No data-coercion: broadband cannot be conditioned on surveillance or data extraction.
Public infrastructure financing

Build with public banking rails

  • Public banking provides low-cost long-term financing for water, broadband, and resilience projects.
  • Open procurement: contracts transparent, auditable, and locally accountable.
  • Local workforce: paid training pipelines and local hiring targets tied to projects.
Resilience & continuity

Plan for shocks without panic

  • Continuity plans for water, power, broadband, and emergency response.
  • Redundancy: microgrids and backup communications where failure costs lives.
  • Climate resilience: flood/heat/wildfire planning integrated by default.
Implementation

First-year build sequence

  1. Universal broadband plan: open-access build strategy prioritized for rural gaps.
  2. Water modernization sprint: publish water dashboards; prioritize high-risk systems.
  3. Fire/EMS baseline standards: equipment + training + mutual aid modernization.
  4. Resilience hubs rollout with local governments and schools.
  5. Public procurement transparency and anti-corruption audits.
