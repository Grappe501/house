Impact Lab

Overview

Universal transportation reduces household costs, expands opportunity, and supports a balanced population distribution by making rural and small-town living more practical while improving mobility inside metros.

Key outcome: less forced car dependence + less congestion + lower energy intensity per trip.
Current system

What we see today

  • Rural isolation for youth, seniors, and workers without reliable vehicles.
  • Urban congestion and high household transportation costs.
  • Patchwork transit systems with poor connections and confusing fares.
  • Energy waste from low-occupancy trips and stop-and-go congestion.
New system

What changes

  • National Mobility Guarantee and mobility hubs everywhere.
  • Express corridor service and regional rail spines where feasible.
  • Frequent metro networks + BRT + integrated first/last mile.
  • Fleet electrification prioritized where it delivers the biggest savings.
Metrics & projections

What we measure

  • Household transportation burden (% of income), by region.
  • Access metrics: time to healthcare, jobs, education, groceries.
  • Ridership, on-time performance, cost per passenger-trip.
  • Energy intensity per passenger-km by mode; emissions trajectory.
  • Population shift signals: rural/small-town stabilization, urban rent pressure changes.
