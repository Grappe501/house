Defense & Service Incentives

Philosophy

Defense exists to protect the people, not to feed a machine

A working-majority republic measures defense success by what it protects: life, stability, sovereignty, and democratic self-determination. We maintain strong deterrence and alliances while reducing waste, profiteering, and open-ended wars.

Core shift: defense is national insurance and resilience—not a permanent growth industry.
Doctrine

Deterrence + resilience + precision

  • Deterrence: credible capability that prevents attack.
  • Resilience: hardened infrastructure, cyber defense, supply-chain security, and disaster readiness.
  • Precision: focus on real threats (state conflict, cyber, trafficking networks, catastrophic terrorism), not endless occupation.

A resilient society is harder to coerce, destabilize, or bankrupt.

Budget logic

Spend for outcomes, not contractors

  • Auditability: every major program publishes cost, schedule, performance, and overruns.
  • Competition rules: prevent single-vendor lock-in; break up monopoly contracting structures.
  • Cost-plus limits: tighten or eliminate contracts that reward delay and bloat.
  • Readiness metrics: fund training, maintenance, and logistics before vanity procurement.
Bright line: no profiteering from war or incarceration. Public money cannot buy private extraction.
Service incentives

Make service the best on-ramp to the middle class

We can incentivize service without coercion by making it a pathway to stability and opportunity:

  • Service dividend: enhanced People’s Wealth Fund dividend tier for completed service terms.
  • Lifetime education: free education already exists—service adds accelerated pathways, stipends, and priority placement.
  • Housing ladder: down payment support + low-cost refinancing via public banking rails.
  • Healthcare stability: guaranteed continuity (including mental health and trauma care).
  • Family support: childcare and spousal job placement assistance.

Service includes military and civil service corps (infrastructure, disaster response, public health, cybersecurity).

Civil Service Corps

A patriotic alternative that strengthens the homeland

  • Infrastructure Corps: grid, water systems, broadband, transportation build-out.
  • Resilience Corps: disaster preparedness, wildfire support, community resilience hubs.
  • Public Health Corps: community clinics, mental health outreach, addiction recovery support.
  • Cyber Corps: public-sector cybersecurity defense and incident response.
Design: national service becomes a respected pathway—like a modern GI Bill—without militarizing society.
Global posture

Strength abroad through legitimacy at home

  • Alliance-centered: work with partners; reduce unilateral overreach.
  • Strategic restraint: avoid open-ended occupations; require clear objectives and sunset clauses.
  • Economic competitiveness: domestic infrastructure + education + health raises national capacity.
  • Standards leadership: lead on privacy, AI safety, and anti-corruption norms.
Integration

How defense ties to the rest of the system

  • Environment & Resilience reduces disaster and instability risk (national security).
  • Universal transportation and broadband improve mobilization and economic strength.
  • Public banking and anti-extraction economics reduce foreign coercion leverage.
