Principle

The land and water are a shared inheritance

A working-majority republic treats the natural commons—air, water, soil, forests, and wildlife—as long-term public wealth. If the commons are polluted, working people pay first: in health costs, lost jobs, lost farms, and lost future.

Goal: protect the commons while supporting productive local economies and fair land use.
Watersheds first

Water protection as a top-tier policy

  • Watershed governance: decisions follow watershed boundaries, not arbitrary lines.
  • Polluter pays: enforce cleanup and long-run monitoring; no externalizing costs onto communities.
  • Public access + safety: protect drinking water sources and public recreation.
  • Transparency: open reporting of discharge, nitrates, PFAS, and major pollutants.
Bright line: no private profit is worth poisoning the public water supply.
Soil and agriculture stewardship

Productivity without depletion

  • Regenerative incentives: support soil-building practices that increase yields long-run.
  • Fair enforcement: protect small farms from being crushed while holding major polluters accountable.
  • Local processing: integrate with food hubs to reduce waste and increase farm viability.
  • Land access: prevent corporate land hoarding that kills local ownership.
Public lands and access

Keep the commons open

  • Public land protection: no quiet sell-offs; major changes require public votes and transparent review.
  • Local stewardship councils: community oversight for land use decisions.
  • Outdoor economy: recreation and conservation create jobs and tourism revenue without extraction.
Pollution and corporate accountability

End the externalization model

  • Stronger penalties: fines must exceed the profit from violation.
  • Criminal liability for fraud: cover-ups and falsified reports treated as serious crimes.
  • Environmental audits: industries with high risk must disclose and be independently audited.
  • Insurance requirements: high-risk operations must carry cleanup insurance and bonding.
Resilience and disaster risk

Prepare communities for the future

  • Flood and wildfire resilience integrated into infrastructure spending.
  • Heat safety plans for workers and vulnerable residents.
  • Resilience hubs serve as local support centers during disasters.

Disaster prevention is cheaper than disaster response.

Implementation

First-year build sequence

  1. Publish watershed dashboards: water quality, discharge sources, and enforcement actions.
  2. Establish watershed councils with citizen oversight and scientific advisory capacity.
  3. Set bonding and insurance rules for high-risk operations; enforce polluter pays.
  4. Expand public access and stewardship programs for public lands.
  5. Integrate resilience into all infrastructure and energy projects.
