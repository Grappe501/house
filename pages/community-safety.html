Community Safety & People’s Police

Philosophy

Safety is legitimacy, not force

A society is safe when the public trusts the system enough to cooperate with it. Force without legitimacy produces resistance, fear, and cycles of escalation. In a working-majority republic, public safety is built on:

  • Local legitimacy (community control and transparency)
  • Prevention (housing, jobs, mental health, addiction treatment)
  • Precision (target violence and trafficking, not poverty)
Core claim: the most effective “policing” is a system that reduces the need for police.
Design

The People’s Police model (community-governed public safety)

We rebuild policing as a public service under local democratic control, with strict standards and transparent outcomes.

  • Local governance board: elected/representative board sets policy, reviews outcomes, controls leadership hiring.
  • Open data: stops, searches, uses of force, complaints, discipline outcomes—published with privacy protections.
  • Independent investigation: officer-involved shootings and serious misconduct investigated externally.
  • Service specialization: mental health response teams, traffic safety, domestic violence specialists, youth outreach.
Bright line: the police do not police themselves.
Community control

How local citizens actually hold power

  • Public safety charter: local “bill of rules” adopted by voters or council with voter ratification.
  • Budget transparency: line-item budgets and contract disclosure.
  • Union and discipline reform: bargaining cannot override accountability standards.
  • Complaint rights: protected complaint filing, rapid timelines, and public reporting.

Community control means policy control, hiring/firing authority, and transparency—not just “advisory committees.”

Federal role limits

No federal forces on the ground (local control rule)

The federal role is support, standards, and resources—not permanent domestic occupation.

  • Default rule: no federal law enforcement or military deployed for routine local policing.
  • Emergency exception: federal assistance can be requested by local leadership under defined conditions, with time limits and local command authority over objectives.
  • Funding leverage: federal grants require transparency, civil rights compliance, training standards, and independent investigations.
  • Focus federal enforcement: corruption, trafficking, organized crime, cross-border and cyber crimes.
Principle: federal presence exists to serve local citizens, under accountable rules.
Prevention stack

The real crime reduction toolkit

  • Housing stability: eviction prevention, housing supply, fair landlord rules.
  • Jobs and wages: local investment, public banking, apprenticeship pipelines.
  • Mental health and addiction: crisis response teams, treatment access, recovery supports.
  • Youth opportunity: after-school programs, paid internships, mentorship, safe spaces.

Crime drops when desperation drops. Policing becomes more precise when communities are stable.

Precision enforcement

What gets priority

  • Violence interruption: targeted interventions against retaliatory cycles.
  • Trafficking and exploitation: human trafficking, wage theft, domestic violence.
  • Gun violence focus: focus on high-risk individuals and illegal trafficking, not mass harassment.
  • Fraud and corruption: protect working people from scams and predatory systems.
Training and standards

Professionalism as a requirement

  • De-escalation, crisis intervention, and bias training.
  • Strict use-of-force continuum with clear consequences.
  • Body-worn camera rules with privacy protections and anti-tampering enforcement.
  • Wellness and fatigue standards (reduces bad outcomes and mistakes).
Integration

How safety connects to justice and prisons

  • Pretrial fairness reduces instability and job loss for low-risk defendants.
  • Sentencing and diversion systems reduce recidivism.
  • Reentry support (housing, jobs, treatment) prevents repeat harm.
