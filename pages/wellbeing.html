Philosophy

Stress is a policy outcome

Most “individual” stress is structural: unstable housing, debt traps, unpredictable schedules, unsafe communities, and inaccessible healthcare. A working-majority republic treats mental and physical health as a national productivity and dignity strategy.

Goal: lower chronic stress at scale by stabilizing the conditions of life.
The stress-reduction stack

Five levers that move the whole society

  • Stability: housing security, predictable bills, debt relief, and fair finance.
  • Time: predictable scheduling, paid leave, and transportation efficiency.
  • Care: free-at-point-of-service healthcare, mental health access, addiction treatment.
  • Belonging: civic participation, community hubs, and local power.
  • Nature + movement: parks, walkability, safe recreation, and healthy food access.
Mental health

Care without stigma or scarcity

  • Universal access to therapy and psychiatric care (with stepped-care pathways).
  • Crisis response teams (not default police) integrated with Community Safety.
  • Addiction treated as a health condition with recovery supports.
  • School-based counseling and family support services.
Bright line: mental health crises are not crimes.
Physical health

Prevention is cheaper than collapse

  • Preventive care as default; chronic disease management supported.
  • Food availability and nutrition supports (ties to Food Justice pillar).
  • Clean air and water enforcement (ties to utilities and resilience).
  • Built environment: sidewalks, safe biking, recreation spaces.
Work and time design

Reduce burnout at the source

  • Predictive scheduling rules and penalties for abusive scheduling.
  • Paid sick leave and caregiving leave.
  • Overtime guardrails and fatigue standards (also improves safety).
  • Training and mobility pipelines so workers can move to better jobs.
Community health infrastructure

Hubs that make support real

  • Neighborhood wellbeing hubs: primary care, counseling, benefits navigation, group programs.
  • Resilience hubs (v6) expanded to include routine community health functions.
  • Public dashboards: wait times, provider access, and outcomes.
