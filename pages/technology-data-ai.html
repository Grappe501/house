Principle

Your data is yours. AI must serve the people.

The 21st-century civil rights fight is about information: who owns it, who profits from it, and who controls people with it. A working-majority republic treats personal data as property and dignity, and treats AI as a powerful tool that must be governed like critical infrastructure.

Goal: protect privacy and freedom while staying globally competitive in AI and technology.
Personal data ownership

Individuals own their personal records

  • Right to access: any organization must provide all personal records on request in usable formats.
  • Right to portability: you can move your data between services without lock-in.
  • Right to deletion: data not required for legal safety obligations can be deleted.
  • Right to audit: you can see who accessed your data, when, and why.
Rule: data use must be explicit, readable, and revocable.
No coercion

It is illegal to discriminate for withholding data

  • Businesses and employers cannot deny services, jobs, pricing, or opportunities because a person refused optional data sharing.
  • “Consent” cannot be bundled into unreadable contracts.
  • Essential services (utilities, healthcare, education, banking rails) cannot be conditioned on surveillance.
Bright line: privacy is a civil right, not a luxury subscription.
Surveillance boundaries

Private spaces are private

  • Private spaces: warrantless surveillance of people in private areas is illegal.
  • Public spaces: observation can occur, but data retention and identification are tightly limited and publicly accountable.
  • Government limits: strict warrant standards, minimization rules, and independent oversight.
  • Corporate limits: bans on secret tracking, dark patterns, and covert biometric capture.

We keep public safety while preventing a permanent surveillance society.

AI governance

Regulate AI without falling behind

  • Tiered risk licensing: the more powerful the model and the higher the impact domain, the stronger the requirements.
  • Safety cases: deployers must document risks, testing, and mitigation before release.
  • Audit rights: independent auditors can test systems for bias, security, and harm.
  • Critical domain rules: healthcare, policing, employment, credit, housing, and voting tech require strict transparency and appeal rights.
Competitive edge: predictable rules + public R&D + open standards create trustworthy innovation.
Automation & jobs

Productivity must be shared

  • Automation dividend: when AI/automation displaces labor, part of productivity gains flows into the People’s Wealth Fund.
  • Transition supports: wage insurance, retraining stipends, and rapid placement pipelines.
  • Worker voice: major automation changes require worker notification, impact assessments, and negotiation.
  • Small business adoption: public tools and training prevent AI from becoming a big-firm moat.

The goal is to raise living standards—not to create a permanent surplus class.

Platform power

End digital monopoly lock-in

  • Interoperability: messaging/social platforms must support open protocols where feasible.
  • Data portability: you can leave without losing your network or history.
  • Algorithm accountability: users can understand why content is shown and opt out of manipulative feeds.
  • Transparent ads: political and behavioral targeting rules are strict, public, and auditable.
Implementation

A pragmatic sequence

  1. Pass a Personal Data Rights Act: ownership, access, portability, deletion, audit trails.
  2. Ban coercive data practices: discrimination for withholding data becomes illegal.
  3. Establish AI Safety & Competition Commission: tiered licensing, audits, and enforcement.
  4. Fund public AI & open standards: keep U.S. competitive without corporate capture.
  5. Automation dividend rails: connect productivity gains to the People’s Wealth Fund.
