Housing Supply & Zoning

Philosophy

Housing is the biggest inflation driver for working people

For most households, “cost of living” is mostly housing. When housing supply lags demand, rents rise, homeownership becomes unreachable, and every wage gain is eaten by landlords and investors.

Tenant protections and anti-hoarding rules are necessary, but they are not sufficient. We also must build enough homes—without displacement and without handing cities to developers.

Goal: abundant, dignified, mixed-income housing so wages translate into stability and ownership.
Non-negotiables

What this model will not do

  • No displacement-first “revitalization.”
  • No deregulation giveaway that enriches developers while communities lose control.
  • No one-size-fits-all zoning mandate that ignores local context.

We use incentives, standards, and accountability to align local control with national outcomes.

Model

Local control + national incentives (a constitutional-capable approach)

The federal role is to set national outcome targets and tie funding to performance—without dictating every local zoning line.

  • Outcome targets: affordability, vacancy, rent-burden, homelessness reduction, and ownership rates.
  • Funding levers: infrastructure, transit, broadband, disaster funds, and community development grants are tied to meeting targets.
  • Local autonomy: localities choose the mix (upzoning, ADUs, infill, mixed-use, land trusts) as long as outcomes improve.
Supply engine

What we build and where

  • Missing middle housing: duplexes, fourplexes, courtyard apartments—especially near job centers and schools.
  • Accessory dwelling units (ADUs): legalized and simplified with safety standards.
  • Infill and rehab: convert vacant buildings, repair blight, and reuse downtown cores.
  • Workforce housing: for teachers, nurses, trades, and public servants.
  • Rural strategies: modular/kit homes, land access, and local infrastructure support.
Abundance is not “luxury towers.” It’s a steady pipeline of ordinary homes normal people can afford.
Anti-displacement protections

Build supply without pushing people out

  • Right-to-return protections in redevelopment zones.
  • Property tax circuit breakers for long-term residents facing rapid value spikes.
  • Anti-eviction abuse rules and relocation assistance in qualified cases.
  • Community land trusts and shared-equity models to keep homes affordable long-term.
Permitting & delivery

Make building faster without corruption

  • Time limits on approvals with automatic escalation steps.
  • Standardized code templates for missing-middle and ADUs to speed compliant builds.
  • Transparent permitting dashboards (time-to-approve, denials, appeals, and reasons).
  • Anti-corruption procurement rules for publicly subsidized projects.
Bright line: faster permitting must not mean weaker safety or developer capture.
Investor capture controls

Stop scarcity profiteering

  • Institutional ownership caps and progressive unit taxes (already in Housing & Homeownership pillar).
  • Vacancy penalties for long-term empty units without legitimate cause.
  • Ban on hidden “algorithmic rent coordination” (collusion by software proxy).
  • Right of first refusal for tenants when properties are sold.
Homeownership expansion

Supply + ownership ladder = working-class wealth

Building supply lowers price pressure. The ownership ladder converts stability into wealth.

  • First-time buyer pipeline (down payment + closing support).
  • Fair mortgage access via the Homeownership Trust and public banking rails.
  • Rent-to-own standards with equity accrual.
  • Co-op and CLT options for permanent affordability.
Transition plan

First year actions that move the needle

  • National Housing Outcomes Dashboard (rent burden, vacancy, homelessness, ownership).
  • Funding alignment: infrastructure/transit/broadband grants tied to outcomes improvements.
  • Permitting dashboard + time-bound approvals for standardized missing-middle and ADU plans.
  • Scale CLTs and rehab funding; create rapid conversion programs for vacant properties.
