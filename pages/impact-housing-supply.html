Impact Lab

Overview

Housing supply is the primary long-run affordability lever. Combined with anti-hoarding rules and tenant protections, supply growth reduces rent inflation, increases homeownership, and stabilizes communities.

Core idea: we control housing inflation by increasing supply and blocking scarcity profiteering—not by freezing markets.
Current system

What we see today

  • Chronic undersupply in growing regions; prices and rents rise faster than wages.
  • Local veto power and slow permitting create artificial scarcity.
  • Investor capture and consolidation amplify rent extraction.
  • Displacement and homelessness rise as stability collapses.
New system

What changes

  • Outcome-based incentives tied to federal funding streams.
  • Fast paths for missing-middle and ADU builds with safety standards.
  • Anti-displacement protections and CLT scaling.
  • Anti-capture rules: ownership caps, vacancy penalties, rent-collusion bans.
Metrics & projections

What we measure

  • Housing starts and completions by type (missing-middle, ADU, rehab, workforce).
  • Vacancy rates, rent growth, and rent-burden distribution.
  • Homeownership rates (overall and first-time buyer rates).
  • Homelessness inflow/outflow and time-to-housing.
  • Displacement indicators and neighborhood stability.
  • Institutional ownership share and vacancy/holding behavior.
