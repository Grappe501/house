Healthcare Governance & Universal Care

Home / The Model / Healthcare
Principle

Healthcare is a public guarantee, delivered locally

A working-majority republic makes healthcare free at the point of service. But universal access is not enough—governance must prevent bureaucracy, shortages, and capture.

Goal: universal care with local delivery, transparent costs, and high quality.
System structure

Universal coverage without corporate rationing

  • Coverage: everyone receives care without premiums, deductibles, or surprise bills.
  • Delivery: public, non-profit, and private providers can operate—but billing to patients is eliminated for covered care.
  • Payment: simplified public payment rails replace fragmented insurance billing.
  • Choice: patients choose providers; providers compete on quality and experience, not denial tactics.

This is not “government medicine.” It is government guarantee with diverse local delivery.

Provider stability

Staffing, rural access, and burnout prevention

  • Rural sustainability: baseline funding for rural hospitals and clinics tied to community need, not profit margins.
  • Workforce pipeline: paid training pathways, loan-free education, and residency expansion.
  • Burnout controls: staffing standards, predictable scheduling, and mental health support for caregivers.
  • Scope modernization: empower nurses, PAs, and community health workers where safe and effective.
Pharma and pricing

End monopoly pricing without killing innovation

  • Negotiated prices: national negotiation for essential drugs and devices.
  • Transparency: R&D claims and pricing markups are audited where public funds are involved.
  • Generic acceleration: shorten abusive exclusivity extensions; enforce anti-evergreening rules.
  • Public option R&D: strategic public funding for high-need areas (antibiotics, rare diseases, public health threats).
Design: pay for real innovation, not monopoly rent.
Quality and accountability

Outcomes, access, and patient rights

  • Wait-time guarantees with published dashboards.
  • Patient bill of rights: clear appeals, record access, and dignity standards.
  • Open procurement: hospitals and systems publish major vendor contracts and conflicts.
  • Fraud enforcement: punish provider fraud and corporate overbilling without punishing patients.
Integration

How this ties into the rest of the republic

  • Wellbeing pillar focuses on stress reduction and prevention.
  • Utilities (water/fire) and food justice reduce health crises upstream.
  • Data rights pillar will govern health data ownership and privacy.
