The civil right

Data dignity and bargaining power

Personal data is currently treated as free raw material. In this model, it becomes negotiated property: people can say yes, no, or “pay me,” with real enforcement.

Required disclosures

No hidden language

  • Plain-language summaries of what data is collected and why.
  • Separate consent for each optional use (no bundling).
  • Time limits on retention and clear deletion mechanisms.
  • Real-time audit logs available to the individual.
Collective bargaining for data

Negotiate as a whole

Where data markets exist, citizens can negotiate collectively (through public standards or data trusts) on: price, permitted uses, and audit requirements.
