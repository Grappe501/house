Philosophy

Resilience is national security

Disasters, climate shocks, and infrastructure failures punish working people first. Resilience is not a luxury; it is economic defense. A strong democracy requires the ability to recover quickly and fairly.

Model

Resilience hubs and hardened infrastructure

  • Community resilience hubs: cooling/heating, power backup, charging, medical coordination, food distribution.
  • Grid hardening: transmission upgrades, microgrids where appropriate, cybersecurity.
  • Water security: clean water protections, infrastructure replacement, contamination accountability.
  • Disaster logistics: pre-positioned supplies and rapid deployment systems.
Equity guardrails

Prevent disaster profiteering

  • Anti-price-gouging and anti-fraud enforcement.
  • Right-to-return and anti-displacement after disasters.
  • Transparent contracting; publish vendors and unit prices.
Rule: disaster recovery must rebuild communities—not extract from them.
Food + housing integration

Resilience is a system

  • Food hubs and local producer stability reduce shock vulnerability.
  • Housing supply and building codes aligned to risk and affordability.
  • Public broadband ensures information and service access during crises.
Transition plan

First-year actions

  • Map critical risk zones and infrastructure fragility.
  • Fund resilience hubs in highest-risk communities first.
  • Launch national procurement transparency dashboard for disaster spending.
