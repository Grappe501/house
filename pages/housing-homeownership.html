Philosophy

Housing is stability infrastructure

Housing is the largest monthly expense for most families and one of the biggest drivers of stress, mobility, and civic participation. A working-majority republic treats housing like utilities: a necessity that cannot be dominated by extraction.

Goal: reduce rent exploitation, increase stable homeownership, and end institutional hoarding.
Universal landlord/tenant rules

One fair baseline everywhere

  • Clear habitability standards: enforceable timelines for repairs and safety.
  • Anti-retaliation: tenants cannot be punished for reporting conditions.
  • Transparent leases: plain-language terms; no hidden fees; standardized disclosure.
  • Predictable rent rules: anti-gouging limits during shortages and disasters.
  • Fair eviction process: due process, mediation first, and emergency protections for families.
Bright line: housing insecurity is treated as a destabilizing risk to the whole economy.
De-incentivize property hoarding

Stop turning neighborhoods into asset portfolios

  • Anti-hoarding taxation: escalating taxes on large-scale absentee ownership portfolios.
  • Institutional caps: limits on corporate acquisition of single-family homes by region.
  • Transparency: beneficial ownership disclosure for property and LLCs.
  • Right of first refusal: tenants or community trusts get first purchase rights in some sales.

The goal is not punishment—it is re-aligning incentives toward local, stable ownership.

Pathways to ownership

A ladder for working families

  • Down payment support: targeted grants or matched savings for first-time buyers.
  • Public banking mortgages: low-fee, transparent mortgages with fair refinancing options.
  • Lease-to-own models: supported by local housing trusts and credit rails.
  • Starter-home build programs: zoning reform + modular + local builders.
Design: shift households from perpetual rent extraction into wealth-building stability.
Community Land Trusts

Keep housing affordable permanently

Community Land Trusts (CLTs) separate land ownership from the building to keep prices affordable across generations.

  • Local governance and anti-speculation covenants.
  • Resale rules that allow wealth-building without runaway price inflation.
  • Works for housing and local commercial space.
Housing supply without displacement

Build more, but protect communities

  • Gentle density: allow ADUs, duplexes, and small multi-family where appropriate.
  • Transit + hub zoning: build around transportation hubs to reduce commute burdens.
  • Anti-displacement: right-to-return and relocation supports for affected residents.
  • Rural housing options: support infill and small-town revitalization tied to universal transportation.
Implementation

First-year build sequence

  1. Publish housing dashboards: rent, vacancy, evictions, corporate ownership share.
  2. Adopt universal landlord/tenant baseline rules with clear enforcement paths.
  3. Launch CLTs and right-of-first-refusal tools in high-extraction zones.
  4. Stand up public banking mortgage rails for transparent, low-fee ownership ladders.
  5. Reform zoning for starter homes and gentle density without displacement.
