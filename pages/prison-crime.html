Prison & Crime System Overhaul

Home / The Model / Prison & Crime
Non-negotiable

No private prisons. Ever.

Incarceration cannot be a profit center. When companies profit from cages, the incentive is to increase imprisonment, extend sentences, and resist rehabilitation.

Rule: no private prisons, no private “detention quota” contracts, and no profit-sharing tied to incarceration volume.
Goal

Decrease crime and decrease imprisonment

This system measures success by:

  • Lower victimization (fewer people harmed)
  • Lower incarceration (fewer people caged)
  • Lower recidivism (fewer repeat offenses)
  • Higher stability (housing, work, health, family continuity)

We stop treating prison as the default tool for social problems.

Crime reduction strategy

Prevention + precision + legitimacy

  • Prevention: housing stability, living wages, addiction treatment, mental healthcare, youth opportunity.
  • Precision enforcement: focus on violence, trafficking, exploitation, corruption—not poverty.
  • Legitimacy: community-governed safety systems increase cooperation and reduce escalation.
Key design: reduce the pipeline into prison by reducing desperation and targeting true harm.
Pretrial justice

Stop punishing poverty

  • No cash-bail default: detention is based on risk, not money.
  • Speedy hearings: rapid review prevents months of pretrial limbo.
  • Defense parity: public defense funded to realistic caseload standards.
  • Alternatives: monitored release, treatment requirements, and restorative pathways where appropriate.
Sentencing redesign

Proportional, evidence-based, and reviewable

  • Violence and exploitation prioritized for incarceration when necessary.
  • Non-violent offenses default toward diversion, treatment, restitution, and supervised alternatives.
  • Second-look review: sentence review mechanisms to adjust excessive terms.
  • End “gotcha” probation: probation should be support, not a trap for technical violations.
Bright line: technical violations should not automatically trigger long imprisonment.
Inside prisons

Rehabilitation as the mission

  • Education and credentials: free inside prisons—literacy to degrees.
  • Treatment: addiction and mental health care as standard, not optional.
  • Work with dignity: paid work with skills training (no forced labor profiteering).
  • Violence prevention: staffing standards, anti-corruption controls, safe grievance systems.

A prison that produces trauma produces more crime. Rehabilitation reduces future harm.

Reentry system

Release is a moment. Reentry is a system.

  • Housing first: stable housing placement before release.
  • Work pipeline: guaranteed job placement support, apprenticeships, and wage pathways.
  • Healthcare continuity: uninterrupted care, especially for addiction and mental health.
  • Record reform: sealing/expungement for eligible offenses after stable time and restitution.
Rule: we do not release people into homelessness and call it “justice.”
Victim-centered justice

Support, restoration, and safety

  • Victim services funded: medical, counseling, relocation where needed.
  • Restorative justice options: for eligible cases where victims choose it.
  • Restitution with realism: repayment plans tied to ability-to-pay, not lifelong debt bondage.
Integration

How this works with the rest of the republic

  • Community Safety & People’s Police provides legitimacy and precision.
  • Poverty floor, housing, and healthcare remove structural crime drivers.
  • Education and automation-transition systems provide real opportunity pathways.
