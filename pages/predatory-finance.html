Philosophy

Why predatory finance must end

Predatory finance is not an accident. It is a business model built on desperation, opacity, and time pressure. When survival requires debt, markets stop allocating capital efficiently and start extracting value from the most vulnerable.

This system does not ban lending. It bans extraction without value.

Targets

What qualifies as predatory

  • Payday and auto-title loans with triple-digit APRs
  • Overdraft fee pyramids and surprise NSF traps
  • Rent-to-own and installment traps without equity accrual
  • Medical debt collection and credit punishment
  • Forced arbitration and fine-print fee harvesting
Rules

The anti-extraction rule set

  • APR caps tied to real cost + modest margin
  • Fee transparency: all-in pricing required
  • No negative-option fees
  • Ability-to-repay standards enforced
  • Ban forced arbitration in consumer finance
Rule: If a product requires confusion to be profitable, it is illegal.
Replacement

What replaces predatory products

  • Public banking option for small-dollar loans
  • Postal-style basic accounts with no overdraft traps
  • Employer-linked emergency credit pools
  • Community credit unions and CDFIs at scale
Outcome

System-wide effects

  • Household stability increases
  • Wages go further without fee leakage
  • Local spending rises
  • Crime and stress-linked health costs fall
