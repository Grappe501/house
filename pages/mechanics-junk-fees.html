Mechanics — Junk Fees

Level C (Mechanics/Proof). Primary mechanics: M4 (Friction-as-Revenue), M5 (Administrative Burden Transfer).

Purpose (what this enforces)

Reduce or eliminate monetized friction in essential access by: (1) constraining fee categories, (2) preventing relabeling/backfill, and (3) ensuring baseline access is not throttled.

Inputs (data + assumptions)

Core inputs

  • avg_fees_per_household (annual)
  • households_count (community/state)
  • capture_rate (share of fees actually reduced after enforcement; 0–1)
  • time_cost_hours (annual hours associated with fee-driven admin loops; proxy)

Act II assumptions used

  • A4: friction can be monetized (complexity & delay create pricing power)
  • A5: administrative burden suppresses participation and increases time poverty

Friction-as-Revenue model

In Act II terms: fees are not random. They are a designed extraction layer—especially where urgency is common.

  • Visibility: make fees legible, comparable, and reportable.
  • Constraint: cap/ban categories most tied to urgency (late fees, “processing,” surprise add-ons).
  • Anti-evasion: treat relabeling as the same category (functional test).

Administrative Burden Transfer

Many fees are paired with paperwork loops: proof, portals, verification, “wrong line/wrong day.” Reducing fees without reducing loops leaves time poverty intact.

  • Consolidate forms and eliminate redundant proofs.
  • Standardize “one-and-done” verification where possible.
  • Publish abandonment rates and time-to-complete as performance metrics.

Failure modes + safeguards

  • Relabeling: safeguard with functional definitions and auditing.
  • Base-price backfill: safeguard with net household burden tracking.
  • Speed throttling: safeguard with minimum service standards for baseline access.
  • Enforcement capture: safeguard with transparency + local reporting and independent review.

Instrumentation (what gets logged, audited, measured)

Minimum instrumentation

  • Fee category reporting (standard taxonomy)
  • Household burden estimates (modeled + surveyed)
  • Service baseline timing (days/hours to access)
  • Abandonment rate on required processes

Tool: Junk Fee Slider (scenario exploration).

Implementation sequencing

  1. Define taxonomy: what counts as a junk fee (functional, not label-based)
  2. Publish baseline: estimate current burden and time cost
  3. Cap/ban: implement constraints and anti-evasion rules
  4. Monitor: track backfill, throttling, and net burden
  5. Iterate: close loopholes; expand baseline public options where needed
