Act I · Room 014

No Villains

Before we go any further, something needs to be said clearly: this house is not built around villains.

That doesn’t mean harm hasn’t been done. It doesn’t mean consequences don’t matter. It means we are resisting the simplest story the mind reaches for when things feel broken.

Blame feels stabilizing. It gives confusion a shape. It lets us imagine that if the “bad people” were removed, the rest would sort itself out. But most of the damage you’ve felt didn’t come from malice. It came from incentives, habits, and systems that kept running long after they stopped making sense.

Most people inside those systems are trying to do something reasonable with the tools they were given. They follow rules they didn’t design. They optimize for metrics they didn’t choose. They make tradeoffs quietly and hope the cost doesn’t land on someone they love.

This isn’t an argument for passivity. It’s an argument for accuracy.

When we skip straight to villains, we miss the architecture. We fight symptoms instead of structures. We replace one cast of characters and leave the stage exactly the same.

So for now, we widen the room. We hold complexity without rushing to judgment. Trust starts here—not by excusing harm, but by understanding how it keeps repeating.
