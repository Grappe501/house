Act I · Room 007

Time Scarcity

There used to be space between things.

Between work and home.
Between effort and rest.
Between one week and the next.

Now it feels compressed.

Time doesn’t disappear all at once.
It fragments.

Ten minutes here.
A half hour there.
Enough to function.
Not enough to recover.

You start measuring life in gaps.
In errands squeezed between obligations.
In evenings that vanish before they begin.

Even rest becomes scheduled.
Optimized.
Protected like a scarce resource.

People say it’s about balance.
But balance assumes there’s room to shift weight.

What happens when there isn’t?

What happens when every hour is already spoken for—by work, by logistics, by the invisible maintenance of staying afloat?

You move faster, not because you want to,
but because slowing down has consequences.

Deadlines don’t wait.
Bills don’t pause.
Fatigue doesn’t register as an excuse.

So the days blur.
The weeks compress.
And the future starts to feel like something you’re always running late for.

This isn’t about poor planning.

It’s about a system that quietly learned how to consume time without announcing the cost.
And once time becomes scarce, everything else follows.
