Self-Blame Engine

Self-Blame Engine.

When something doesn’t work, the system offers feedback.

It arrives as guidance. A suggestion. A tip for improvement. Language that sounds supportive, even encouraging.

You are told how to do better next time. Which steps to review. Where you might have misunderstood the process.

Nothing indicates a flaw in the structure itself. The instructions assume the system is sound. If there is friction, it must be coming from you.

You try again, adjusting your approach. You read more carefully. You follow the steps in the recommended order.

When the outcome does not change, the explanation narrows. Perhaps you missed something. Perhaps you need more practice. Perhaps this path simply requires a higher level of familiarity.

The idea settles quietly.

Others seem to move through without difficulty. They must understand something you do not.

Soon, the question shifts. Not Why is this happening? But What am I doing wrong?

The system continues to function as designed. It measures. It responds. It records.

And because it never names itself as the source of strain, it teaches you to look inward for every explanation, even when the pressure was built into the process all along.
