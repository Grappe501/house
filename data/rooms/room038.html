Private Hallways

Private Hallways.

Not everyone walks the same way through the building.

Some corridors are quieter. They move faster, bend around corners you do not pass, connect rooms that seem otherwise far apart. You notice them only in glimpses—an opening that closes, a door you did not realize was there.

You assume they are for convenience. A way to reduce congestion. A design choice meant to help things run smoothly.

Access is not denied exactly. It is simply unnecessary for most people. Your route works well enough.

Occasionally, you see someone step aside and disappear down one of these hallways. When they return, they seem unchanged, though their timing is better. Their tasks are already complete.

No announcement explains the difference. No sign suggests you are missing anything.

The main corridor remains open and functional. You continue along it with others like you, sharing space, moving at a familiar pace.

Over time, the building begins to feel larger. Distances stretch. Some rooms feel harder to reach than they once did.

You learn that certain matters are handled elsewhere. Not here. Not on this path.

And because your hallway is the one you know, it starts to feel like the whole building, even as other passages quietly shape what you never encounter.
