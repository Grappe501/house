Sorting Hat

Sorting Hat.

It happens without ceremony.

A question is asked, then another. Some answers are required. Others are optional. You are told this is only to help the process work better.

Somewhere beyond your view, information is arranged. Boxes are checked. Fields are filled. A category is assigned.

No one tells you what the categories mean. They are described as neutral, technical, necessary. They help route requests efficiently.

You notice small differences afterward. Which forms you see first. How long certain explanations take. Which options appear expanded, and which remain collapsed.

Nothing about this feels personal. That may be the point.

You are not judged. You are sorted.

Once sorted, the system responds consistently. People like you tend to receive things in a particular order. Processes move at a familiar speed. Expectations are calibrated before you arrive.

You could ask to be reconsidered. The option exists. But the path to review is longer, and the benefits unclear.

Most people accept the category they are given. It explains the experience well enough.

And once explained, it becomes easier not to question why the system treats you the way it does.
