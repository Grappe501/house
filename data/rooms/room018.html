Not a Conspiracy

When patterns start to appear, the mind looks for intent.

It’s tempting to believe that what you’re seeing must be the result of a hidden plan, a coordinated effort, a group pulling strings behind the walls. That explanation at least offers clarity. Someone is in control.

But most of what’s been accumulating here didn’t require a conspiracy. It required alignment. Incentives pointing in the same direction. Small decisions made repeatedly under pressure. Systems that rewarded speed over care, efficiency over understanding, growth over stability.

In an attic, nothing arrives all at once. Boxes get stacked. Old tools are set aside. Temporary fixes become permanent. Over time, the weight builds quietly overhead.

This is why looking for villains can be misleading. It narrows the story too much. The truth is wider and more uncomfortable: many of these outcomes were predictable, even rational, given the structures in place.

Recognizing that doesn’t excuse harm. It changes the work. Instead of hunting for secret meetings, you start examining feedback loops. Instead of arguing about motives, you trace incentives and consequences.

What’s been built can be understood. And what’s been understood can be redesigned.
