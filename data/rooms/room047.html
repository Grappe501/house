Legitimacy Gap

Legitimacy Gap.

The signs are impressive.

Names are engraved. Titles are displayed. Certifications line the walls in careful order.

You are told this is how trust is established. Credentials signal competence. Position confirms responsibility.

And yet, something feels misaligned.

The people making decisions seem distant from the effects of those decisions. The explanations sound practiced, but incomplete.

You follow the rules. You respect the process. You acknowledge the authority of the structure.

Still, when outcomes arrive, they do not always carry the weight the signs promised.

The gap is subtle. Not a rejection of legitimacy, but a growing uncertainty about where it actually comes from.

Is it granted by position? By procedure? By results?

The system does not clarify. It continues to operate as if the answer is settled.

You adjust accordingly. You comply, but with less conviction. You listen, but with quiet reservation.

Over time, respect becomes conditional. Trust is extended carefully, in smaller amounts.

And because the structure insists on its own authority, the distance between what is declared and what is believed widens without being named, leaving you navigating a space where legitimacy feels assumed, but no longer fully earned.
