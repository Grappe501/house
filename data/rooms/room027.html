Act II · Room 027

Procedure

The next room doesn’t feel like a room.

It feels like a process.

Not harsh. Not dramatic. Just a sequence of steps that appear reasonable on their own. The kind of steps people stop noticing once they’ve done them enough times.

Sign here.
Confirm that.
Acknowledge the terms.
Update the form.

Procedure has a calming effect. It creates the impression that someone has already thought it through. That the rules were built for safety. That if you follow the steps, things will work the way they’re supposed to.

And most of the time, you do follow them. Because resisting a procedure feels like resisting common sense. It feels like being difficult. Like wasting everyone’s time.

But procedure also does something quieter: it converts questions into paperwork. It turns “why” into “did you complete the steps.” It shifts attention away from outcomes and toward compliance.

When a system wants to change without a fight, it rarely argues. It standardizes.
