Scoreboard

Every system keeps score.

Sometimes it’s obvious—profit and loss, test scores, arrest numbers, approval ratings. Sometimes it’s quieter: performance reviews, “engagement,” productivity, compliance. But underneath, the logic is the same.

What gets measured becomes what gets protected. What gets rewarded becomes what gets repeated. What gets ignored becomes invisible—no matter how real it feels inside a person’s body.

This is why a scoreboard is never neutral. It doesn’t just report the game. It teaches everyone what the game is.

In a healthy house, the score reflects the people inside it—stability, dignity, time to breathe, outcomes that match effort. In a distorted one, the score drifts toward what’s easy to count, what’s profitable to optimize, what looks good on paper.

And once the scoreboard is installed, it starts changing behavior. People adapt. They chase the number. They learn what gets noticed. They learn what doesn’t.

If you want to understand power, start by asking who chose the scoreboard—and what they decided not to count.
