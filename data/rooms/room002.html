Name the Weight

Sometimes the hardest part is not carrying it.

It’s pretending you’re not.

We learn early to keep moving.
To stay useful.
To handle what’s in front of us.
To be grateful for what we have, even when the cost is hidden in our bodies.

So we translate weight into personal failure.
We call it laziness.
We call it disorganization.
We call it not wanting it badly enough.

But listen—many of the people who feel the most weight are the ones who are doing the most carrying.

They’re showing up.
They’re doing the math.
They’re making the calls.
They’re stretching the groceries, the hours, the patience, the sleep.

And still, it can feel like the floor shifts under your feet in slow motion.

This is not a diagnosis.
It’s a name.

Because once something has a name, it stops being a private shame.
It becomes a shared signal.
