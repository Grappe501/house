Learned Helplessness

Learned Helplessness.

At first, you try.

You follow the steps. You submit the request. You explain the issue clearly, then more carefully.

Sometimes there is a response. Sometimes there is not. When there is, it sounds familiar, as if it has been written before.

You make adjustments. You take the feedback seriously. You approach the process with patience.

The outcome remains the same.

Not always immediately. Not dramatically. Just enough repetition to teach a pattern.

Eventually, the effort begins to feel disconnected from the result. Whether you push or pause, comply or question, the system responds within a narrow range.

You notice yourself hesitating before acting. Not out of fear, but calculation. Is this worth the energy? Will anything change?

Small problems go unreported. Larger ones are delayed. You adapt by lowering expectations.

This feels like maturity. Like learning how things really work.

You stop thinking in terms of possibility and start thinking in terms of endurance.

And because nothing explicitly tells you to give up, it feels less like surrender and more like realism— a quiet agreement with limits that were never clearly stated.
