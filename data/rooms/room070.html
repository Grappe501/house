Cure Time

Let the joint set before you load it.

Some failures happen not because the design was wrong, but because it was rushed.

Materials need time to bind. Joints need time to set. Pressure applied too early breaks connections that would have held if left alone.

Cure time is rarely visible. It looks like inactivity. It produces no immediate signal.

From the outside, nothing appears to be happening. From inside the structure, everything depends on this pause.

When cure time is skipped, later failures are often blamed on weakness of material or lack of commitment.

The joint cracks. The load shifts. The build is declared unworkable.

In reality, the structure was never allowed to become what it was designed to be.

Cure time is not patience as virtue. It is patience as physics.

It cannot be negotiated. It cannot be accelerated without cost.

This pause does not signal retreat. It signals readiness for load— once the conditions are right.

For now, nothing needs to be tested. Nothing needs to be proven.

The work here is simply to let the joint hold, so that when weight returns, it does not turn care into damage.

The structure remains. Quietly setting.
