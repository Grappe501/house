Act I · Room 001

The Threshold

There’s a moment most people recognize, even if they’ve never named it.

It comes late at night, when the house is finally quiet.
The lights are low.
The day has finished asking things of you.
And for a brief stretch of time, nothing is required.

That’s when the questions arrive—not the loud ones, not the political ones, but the smaller, steadier ones that don’t demand answers right away.

Why does this feel heavier than it used to?
Why does effort stretch thinner now?
Why does stability feel closer one year and farther the next?

Most people don’t say these questions out loud.
They don’t need to.

They live with them.
Carry them.
Set them down and pick them back up again, the way you do with anything that’s become part of daily life.

We tend to assume these feelings are personal.
That they come from individual choices.
Personal shortcomings.
Bad timing.
Not quite doing enough of the right things in the right order.

But before deciding what they mean, it’s worth pausing long enough to notice that they exist at all.

This book isn’t here to argue with you.
It isn’t here to tell you what to believe.
And it isn’t here to rush you toward a conclusion.

It’s here to slow the moment down.

To stand with you at the edge of something familiar and look at it carefully—not to tear it apart, but to understand how it holds together.

Think of this as a threshold.

You don’t have to step through it yet.
You only have to notice that you’re standing in front of it.
