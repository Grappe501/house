Personalization Trap

It starts innocently: the world gets a little easier when it starts speaking your name.

The feed learns your preferences, your habits, your anger triggers, the kind of stories that keep you scrolling. It feels like attention. It feels like care. But it’s not really you being understood—it’s you being profiled.

Personalization is a trap because it turns public life into a mirror maze. Each of us is shown a slightly different version of reality, tuned for engagement, tuned for retention, tuned for ad value. Two neighbors can live in the same town and occupy two different worlds. They don’t just disagree on conclusions—they disagree on what happened.

And the most dangerous part is the emotional logic: when something is tailored to us, we feel it’s earned. “This is my feed.” “These are my facts.” “These are my people.” The house starts rearranging itself while we’re still walking through it.

That’s why the project has to be built like a common room, not a private hallway. We need shared artifacts: the same base facts, the same definitions, the same evidence trails. If we can’t stand in the same room together, we can’t rebuild anything together.

So when the system tries to hand you a perfectly customized story, pause. Ask what it’s costing you: not money—community. Not convenience—consent. Not entertainment—shared reality.
