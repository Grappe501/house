Room 5 — The Bedroom

Who you are here.

The bedroom is quieter than the others.

Not in the way a room gets quiet when sound fades, but in the way a space feels hushed out of respect, as if it expects something fragile to happen inside it. The hum is still there, but it’s distant now, pulled back beneath the floorboards, softened to a presence you feel more than hear.

The light is low and diffuse, coming from places I can’t immediately identify. No single fixture announces itself. The room seems to glow rather than shine.

The bed sits against the far wall, neatly made.

Not hotel-perfect. Not untouched. The sheets are smooth but not tight, the pillow slightly indented on one side, as if someone rested their head there briefly and then thought better of it. The blanket is folded halfway down, an invitation paused mid-gesture.

I step inside and close the door behind me.

The click is quieter here.

The room smells faintly of clean fabric and warm wood. It’s subtle enough that I might have imagined it, but comforting all the same. The air feels heavier, thicker, like it’s meant to hold me in place.

On one side of the bed is a nightstand. On it rests a book.

The cover is plain. No image. No decoration. Just a title, printed neatly in the same calm font I’ve seen elsewhere in the house:

Who You Are Here

I don’t pick it up right away.

Across from the bed is a wardrobe, its doors closed. They’re taller than I expect, stretching nearly to the ceiling. The handles are worn smooth, as if by frequent use. When I open one, I find clothing arranged carefully inside.

Everything is my size.

Not exactly what I would choose, but close enough to feel intentional. Shirts in neutral colors. Pants pressed but comfortable. Shoes lined up on the bottom shelf, practical and clean.

I run my fingers along a sleeve. The fabric is soft, familiar. Something about it makes my chest tighten, like recognition without memory.

I notice then that the clothes are grouped by occasion.

At Home
At Work
Out

Small tags stitched discreetly into the hangers.

I close the wardrobe.

The mirror hangs on the wall beside it, framed in the same dark wood I’ve seen elsewhere. It’s positioned at a height that catches my reflection naturally, without effort. I step closer.

For a moment, it’s just me.

My face looks tired, but not alarmed. My shoulders sit slightly higher than usual. My jaw is set in a way I don’t remember choosing. I look… composed.

Then the reflection shifts.

Not dramatically. Not enough to break the illusion. Just small adjustments—my posture straightens a fraction more, my expression settles into something neutral and agreeable. The change is subtle enough that I wonder if it’s my imagination correcting itself.

I raise a hand.

The reflection raises its hand too.

But it feels delayed, like it waited to see how I would move before deciding how to respond.

I step back.

The bed draws my attention again. It looks comfortable. Inviting in the way of something that promises rest without asking for much in return. I sit on the edge.

The mattress yields immediately, cradling me without swallowing me whole. My body responds before I can think, shoulders loosening, breath deepening. The room seems to exhale with me.

I lie back.

The ceiling above is smooth and unadorned. No cracks. No fixtures. Just a soft surface that reflects the low light evenly. I stare at it, waiting for something to happen.

Nothing does.

Time stretches.

I’m aware of my own breathing, the slow rise and fall of my chest. Each breath feels encouraged, like the room is pleased by my stillness. The hum fades further, almost imperceptible now.

A thought drifts through my mind, uninvited but gentle: You could stay here.

It doesn’t feel like a suggestion. More like an observation.

I sit up suddenly, the movement sharper than necessary. The feeling of rest evaporates, replaced by a faint unease—an awareness that I was slipping into something without noticing the edges.

I glance at the nightstand again.

The book waits where it was.

This time, I pick it up.

It’s lighter than I expect. The pages are thin, the text printed in short, measured lines. I flip it open at random.

You do well when you are consistent.
Others appreciate reliability.
Rest comes easier when expectations are met.

I turn another page.

You are calmer when you follow through.
You belong when you contribute.
You are at your best when you don’t make things difficult.

The words are familiar. Not quoted from anything I recognize, but shaped like thoughts I’ve had before. Thoughts that always seemed to arrive on their own.

I close the book and set it back down.

My hand lingers on the cover longer than necessary.

The mirror catches my eye again. I look, half-expecting the reflection to have changed while I wasn’t watching.

It hasn’t.

But I have.

There’s a heaviness in my limbs now, a tiredness that feels earned, even though I can’t name what I’ve done to deserve it. My body wants rest. My mind wants permission.

I realize, with a quiet start, that the light in the room never fully dims. It softens, yes, but it doesn’t go dark. There is no true night here.

Sleep would be possible.

Rest would be conditional.

I stand and move back toward the door. The floor beneath my feet is warm, comforting in a way that makes leaving feel unnecessary. My hand rests on the knob.

For a moment, I hesitate.

The room behind me is prepared. The bed waits. The clothes are ready. The book knows who I am here.

I open the door.

The hallway greets me with its steady light and orderly silence. The hum returns, familiar now, almost reassuring.

As I step out of the bedroom, I have the distinct, unsettling sense that something has been left behind—not an object, but a version of myself that would have fit very well in that room.

The door closes softly.

Behind it, the bedroom remains exactly as it was.

Waiting.
