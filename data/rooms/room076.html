Act IV — Maintenance (INTEGRATE) • Room 076

Knock

Start with consent, not conquest.

The work begins smaller than most people expect.

Not with a plan. Not with a pitch. Not with an announcement that something is about to change.

It begins at a door.

A knock is an ethical shape. It admits the boundary. It acknowledges that you are outside.

A knock does not assume entry. It does not test loyalty. It does not demand attention.

It simply makes presence known and waits for consent to be given.

This is the first difference between maintenance and conquest.

Conquest treats people like terrain. It treats resistance as something to overcome. It interprets silence as permission.

Maintenance treats people like neighbors. It treats boundaries as real. It understands that refusal does not make someone an obstacle.

A knock also protects the person doing the knocking. It keeps you from turning urgency into intrusion. It keeps you from confusing certainty with entitlement.

The house you are trying to keep standing is not only walls and beams. It is the shared agreement that no one is required to surrender themselves in order to belong.

This is why the first action is not a demand. It is contact that respects capacity.

A knock can be answered later. Or not at all.

The point is not to force a response. The point is to begin in a way that does not create damage simply by arriving.

If this act is going to hold, it must start here: at the boundary, with consent, with a human-scale invitation that keeps dignity intact.
