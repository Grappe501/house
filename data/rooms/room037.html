Filtered Neighbors

Filtered Neighbors.

At first, it feels like proximity.

The people around you seem familiar. They speak in recognizable tones. They share concerns that echo your own.

You pass them often, or at least it feels that way. Their names appear regularly. Their faces are easy to recall. Conversation moves smoothly, without the need for explanation.

You assume this is coincidence. A natural clustering of interests, schedules, preferences.

Only later do you notice who you do not see.

There are doors you never pass. Voices that do not reach this corridor. Perspectives that arrive already summarized, if they arrive at all.

Nothing has been hidden deliberately. The system is simply efficient. It connects you with people it believes you will recognize.

This makes things easier. Disagreement is rare. Friction dissolves before it forms.

When something unfamiliar appears, it feels out of place. Not wrong—just distant, harder to situate.

You tell yourself the building is large. That everyone has their own wing. That what you encounter is only a slice of a much wider structure.

And yet, over time, the slice begins to feel like the whole.

The neighbors you know start to define what normal looks like, simply because they are the ones you are shown.
