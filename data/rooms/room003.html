Act I · Room 003

Shared Experience

If this were only happening to you, it would feel different.

It would feel sharper.
More personal.
Easier to explain away as a mistake or a misstep or a season you just need to get through.

But that’s not what most people notice.

What they notice is how often the same phrases show up in different mouths.
Different towns.
Different jobs.
Different lives that aren’t supposed to resemble one another.

People say they’re tired in the same way.
They describe the same calculations.
They make the same quiet tradeoffs and pretend they didn’t notice.

You hear it at kitchen tables.
In passing comments at work.
In jokes that land a little too close to the truth.

It shows up when someone pauses before answering a simple question about how things are going.
When a conversation drifts toward money, time, or the future—and then drifts away again.

Not because the answers are dramatic.
But because they’re shared.

There’s a strange comfort in that realization, even when it carries a hint of sadness.

It means you didn’t imagine it.
It means the feeling didn’t come from nowhere.
And it means you’re not carrying this alone, even if it’s felt that way.

This isn’t about comparison.
It’s not about ranking struggle or measuring who has it harder.

It’s about noticing a pattern.

And patterns, once noticed, have a way of changing what we’re able to see next.
