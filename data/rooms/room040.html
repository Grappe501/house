Act II · Room 040

Door You Didn’t Choose

Door You Didn’t Choose.

You pass it without stopping.

It does not call attention to itself. No light flashes. No sign suggests importance. It looks like any other door you decided not to open.

You assume it leads somewhere irrelevant. A storage room. A service corridor. A space meant for someone else.

The decision feels small. You make dozens like it every day. This one barely registers.

Later, you cannot remember when you first walked past it. Only that it has always been closed when you noticed.

Nothing prevents you from turning back. The door remains available. But it would require retracing steps, explaining why you changed course, interrupting a flow that has already carried you forward.

So you keep moving.

Over time, the unopened door becomes part of the background. It blends in with the walls. It stops feeling like a choice at all.

You do not feel regret. There is no sense of loss, exactly. Just a faint awareness that something else exists, untouched, unexplored, continuing without you.

And because you never saw what was inside, it is easy to believe that nothing important was missed.
