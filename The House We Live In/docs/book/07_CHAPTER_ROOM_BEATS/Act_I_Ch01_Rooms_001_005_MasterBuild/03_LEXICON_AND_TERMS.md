# Chapter 01 Lexicon — Terms & Phrases (Controlled Introduction)

This chapter introduces language that will later carry technical meaning **without feeling technical yet**.

## Principles
- Introduce only what the reader can hold calmly.
- Prefer ordinary words with layered meaning.
- Avoid jargon, ideology, and “diagnosis” language.

## Approved terms (seed list)
### Consent / Choice language
- “You can stop here.”
- “Nothing happens if you don’t.”
- “There’s no rush.”
- “You’re only at the door.”

**Why:** establishes agency and reduces vigilance.

### Presence language
- “Where you are.”
- “Right now.”
- “This is not a test.”
- “Attention is enough.”

**Why:** disarms performance anxiety; sets participation rules.

### Containment language
- “Held”
- “A shape”
- “Inside”
- “Rooms”

**Why:** primes “systems are containers” without critique.

### House language
- “You can think of it as a house.”
- (Only once in Room 004)

**Why:** gives a shared noun for later expansion.

## Blacklist (Act I)
Avoid these in Rooms 001–005:
- “trap,” “labyrinth,” “cage,” “prison”
- “compliance,” “obedience,” “inspection,” “certificate”
- “system,” “machine,” “operating system” (belongs in Act II)
- moralizing verbs: “should,” “must,” “wake up,” “see the truth”

## Word-choice discipline (micro rules)
- Prefer **concrete nouns** over abstract nouns.
- Prefer **short sentences** to set tempo in Rooms 001–002.
- Avoid rhetorical questions until Room 005 (and only one, if used).
- If you need a charged adjective, soften with a human qualifier (“a little,” “almost,” “barely”).
