# Room 002 — Blueprint (10× MasterBuild)
**Chapter 01 / Rooms 001–005**  
**Room Function:** ORIENTATION / MIRROR  
**Act:** I (Permission)

> Room 2’s job is to **build trust through recognition**.
> The reader should feel: *“I know this place.”*
> Not: *“I’m being led.”*

---

## A. Room 002 Prime Directive

### What this room must accomplish
1) **Confirm safety** established in Room 1 without repeating the consent language.
2) Offer **familiarity**: patterns, objects, domestic logic, everyday physics.
3) Create the first “mirror”: the house reflects the reader’s lived world without naming it.
4) Quietly establish **baseline normal** so Room 3 can introduce the first distortion.

### What this room must NOT do (Act I containment)
- No ominous framing
- No surveillance vibes
- No “system” language
- No accusation, no diagnosis
- No explicit power analysis
- No “the point is…”

Room 2 is not a reveal. It is a **settling-in**.

---

## B. Emotional Target

### Reader state
- **Soft curiosity**
- **Low vigilance**
- **A small, involuntary recognition** (the mirror)

### Emotional temperature
- Slightly warmer than Room 1
- Not cozy; just *human*
- The house feels inhabited by “ordinary life,” even if no one is seen

**Success signal:** the reader relaxes a fraction.

---

## C. Sensory Palette (Color / Light / Sound / Motion)

### Color
- Warm neutrals (bone / oatmeal / faded tan / soft wood)
- No harsh blacks or clinical whites yet
- Avoid “blue dread” tones (save for later Acts)

### Light
- Natural, indirect light
- A suggestion of late afternoon or early morning — time that feels lived-in
- Light should not “flicker” in Act I

### Sound (optional ambience)
- Barely-there house noise: settling wood, distant air movement
- No mechanical hum dominance here (Room 1 can carry hum; Room 2 should feel more domestic)
- Silence is allowed, but it should feel **earned**, not imposed

### Screen motion / UI drift
- Minimal
- Slow, almost unnoticeable
- The room should never “perform” at the reader

---

## D. Symbol Set (Allowed Load)

Room 2 introduces **baseline domestic symbols** that later become loaded.

### Primary symbols (safe in Act I)
- **Hallway / threshold beyond the door**
- **A small table / hook / bowl / key dish** (ordinary place for ordinary things)
- **Scuffed floor / worn runner** (evidence of repetition)
- **Light switch that works** (normal physics, no horror tropes)
- **A smell the reader can name** (soap, dust, old wood, clean linen)

### Metaphor expansion discipline
Room 2 does NOT turn objects into thesis.
Objects remain objects — *with a faint aura of meaning*.

**Rule:** If the prose “explains” a symbol, it’s too soon.

---

## E. Language Rules (Word Choice Discipline)

### Allowed diction
- Simple nouns
- Sensory verbs
- Familiar domestic adjectives (“worn,” “clean,” “soft,” “quiet,” “plain”)

### Avoid / blacklist (Room 2)
- trap, system, mechanism, compliance, control
- surveillance, watched, observed
- fate, destiny, chosen
- “you always…” (over-generalizing the reader)

### Terms that may be introduced (lightly)
If we introduce any term in Room 2, it must be **ordinary** and non-ideological:
- “hallway”
- “runner”
- “doorframe”
- “hinge”
- “entryway”

No special glossary terms yet.

---

## F. Rhythm & Pacing Recipe

### Sentence rhythm
- Slightly longer than Room 1 (more descriptive, more embodied)
- Still breathable; whitespace remains generous

### Paragraph structure
- 1–2 sentence paragraphs
- One “anchor line” paragraph (short, grounding)
- End with a soft handoff, not a hook

### Audio pause targets (if narration exists)
- Small pauses after object-noticing lines (gives reader time to “see”)
- No dramatic pauses (Act I avoids theatrics)

---

## G. Micro-Beat Map (Internal Spine)

### Beat 1 — Crossing (but not committing)
- The reader steps *just inside*
- The house remains non-reactive
- The door’s existence fades slightly into background (do not repeat “door” too much)

### Beat 2 — The ordinary inventory
- A few objects that imply ordinary habits
- Wear patterns that imply time, repetition, routine

### Beat 3 — The mirror moment (recognition)
- A single line that triggers: *“I’ve lived this.”*
- Not nostalgia; recognition

### Beat 4 — Baseline normal locked
- The room stabilizes into “this is how things are here”
- Sets the platform for Room 3 to break one expectation

### Beat 5 — Exit vector (toward Room 3)
- A subtle directional pull: a hallway bend, a half-open interior door, a sound that might be nothing
- **No dread**
- The reader should want to keep going because it feels safe enough to do so

---

## H. Handoff Logic

### From Room 1 → Room 2
Room 1 established consent. Room 2 establishes **belonging** (without claiming the reader).

### Room 2 → Room 3
Room 2 must create a clean baseline so Room 3 can introduce exactly one fracture.

**If Room 2 is already weird, Room 3 has nowhere to go.**

---

## I. “Why Each Word” Discipline (Editorial Control)

Room 2 is where you can accidentally over-write. Use this control:

- Every adjective must do one job:
  - sensory specificity, or
  - emotional warmth, or
  - lived-in time

If an adjective does not do one of those, remove it.

- Every metaphor must be **optional**:
  - If the metaphor is required to understand the sentence, it’s too early.
  - If the sentence stands without it, keep it.

---

## J. Acceptance Gates (Room 2)

A Room 2 draft is approved only if:

1) **Recognition Gate:** At least one moment triggers “familiar,” not “strange.”
2) **Warmth Gate:** The emotional temperature rises slightly from Room 1.
3) **Containment Gate:** No thesis language, no explanation of symbols.
4) **Baseline Gate:** The room feels normal enough that Room 3 can fracture it.
5) **Agency Gate:** The reader still feels free to stop reading.

---

## K. Drafting Starter Prompts (for the prose pass)

When we write Room 2 prose, we will use prompts like:
- “Name three ordinary objects that imply ordinary routines.”
- “Describe wear patterns without interpreting them.”
- “Create one line of recognition that is not nostalgia.”
- “End with a directional cue that is not a hook.”

Room 2 is the calm that makes later disturbance credible.
