Room 2 — The Entryway

Nothing asks anything of you.

Inside, the house feels much like you expected it would.

There’s a small entryway just beyond the door. Nothing about it stands out. A narrow table sits against the wall, its surface mostly clear except for a few ordinary things—items placed down and picked up again so many times they’ve stopped being noticed.

The floor shows signs of use. Not damage, exactly, just the quiet evidence of repetition. A runner has shifted slightly over time, its edge curling where feet have brushed past it.

The light is steady. It doesn’t flicker or hum. It simply fills the space enough to see where you are.

The air smells faintly of something familiar. Clean, but not new. Like wood that’s been wiped down more than once.

You don’t feel rushed here. There’s room to stand without being in the way. Room to pause.

It’s the kind of space designed for coming and going, not for staying. And yet, it holds you for a moment longer than necessary.

Nothing asks anything of you.

Still, without quite knowing why, you notice where you’ve placed your feet.
