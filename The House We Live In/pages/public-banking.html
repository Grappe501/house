Philosophy

Banking is infrastructure, not a tollbooth

In a working-majority republic, the basic ability to store money, pay bills, borrow small amounts, and smooth emergencies is not a luxury product. When core banking is controlled primarily by profit-maximizing intermediaries, households become fee farms and communities lose the ability to invest locally.

This model does not eliminate private banking. It establishes a public utility layer that guarantees fair access and forces the entire market to compete on service—not extraction.

Core claim: a society cannot be economically free if it must rent access to money.
Design

The Public Banking Option (PBO)

The PBO is a national public option for everyday finance, delivered through accessible channels (online + local storefront partners), with three functions:

  • Fair accounts: no overdraft traps, transparent fees (ideally zero), instant bill pay, and wage deposit access.
  • Small-dollar credit: emergency loans and bridge financing at capped APR, tied to ability-to-repay.
  • Local lending engine: wholesale funding and guarantees for community lenders (credit unions/CDFIs) and small business lending.

Think of it like a public road system: private businesses still exist, but everyone is guaranteed a safe path to participate.

Mechanics

How it de-powers Wall Street without “destroying” it

  • Competition by default: when a fair public option exists, predatory fee models lose customers.
  • Liquidity with rules: PBO provides stable funding to local/community lenders—so regions aren’t hostage to big-bank priorities.
  • Lower fragility: households with stable accounts and emergency credit are less likely to default, get evicted, or spiral into collections.
  • Generational shift: over time, the share of national cashflow going to junk fees and interest traps collapses, while productive investment grows.
Bright line: the goal is to collapse extraction, not capital formation.
Products

What the PBO offers (baseline menu)

  • Safe Checking: no overdraft; optional “decline at zero” default; real-time balance alerts.
  • Safe Savings: automatic rounding or payroll splits; small match for low-income (optional, later).
  • Emergency Credit: small loans (e.g., $300–$2,000) with low APR, fixed repayment, no rollovers.
  • Bill Smoothing: optional utility/rent smoothing tools that prevent late-fee spirals.
  • Student & training supports: financing only for non-tuition costs where needed, since education is free.

All products are plain-language, with standardized disclosures. No forced arbitration.

Integration

How it connects to the rest of the system

  • Predatory finance replacement: PBO is the default alternative to payday/overdraft traps.
  • Mortgage conversion support: the PBO can service refinanced mortgages and simplify payment stability tools.
  • Small business growth: guarantees and wholesale credit expand local lending and business formation.
  • Disaster resilience: rapid emergency disbursements and payment relief during regional shocks.
Safeguards

How we prevent corruption, politicization, or inefficiency

  • Independent governance: chartered utility-style board with strict conflict-of-interest rules.
  • Transparency: publish fee schedules, approval rates, lending geography, and performance metrics.
  • Risk controls: conservative underwriting for small-dollar credit; loss reserves; independent audits.
  • Anti-capture: lobbying restrictions and revolving-door limits for PBO leadership.
Transition plan

How we launch without breaking the current system

  1. Phase 1: Safe accounts and payment rails (immediate consumer relief).
  2. Phase 2: Emergency credit + small business guarantees (replace predatory products).
  3. Phase 3: Expand wholesale funding and partner networks; integrate with broader debt reset and housing systems.

Private banks continue to operate. They simply must compete against a fair baseline that makes extraction unprofitable.
