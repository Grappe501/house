Principle

Energy is a public utility layer—priced for stability

Energy is the bloodstream of the economy. When energy prices spike, working families suffer first and hardest. A working-majority republic designs energy policy for price stability, reliability, and long-run independence—not for extraction.

Goal: reliable, affordable energy for all; modern grid; pragmatic decarbonization without shocks.
Energy stability first

Protect households and small businesses

  • Price stability rules: anti-gouging enforcement and transparent rate-setting.
  • Affordability floors: extreme-weather protections and burden caps for low-income households.
  • Reliability standards: utilities must meet outage and restoration benchmarks.
  • Local accountability: community oversight boards for utilities and major rate cases.
Grid modernization

Build the 21st-century grid

  • Transmission build-out: reduce congestion and connect diverse generation.
  • Distribution upgrades: modern transformers, wildfire protection, undergrounding where cost-effective.
  • Storage and flexibility: batteries, pumped storage, demand response, microgrids.
  • Cyber resilience: treat grid security as national security.
Design: a modern grid is the backbone of universal broadband, transportation, and industry.
Pragmatic transition

No shock therapy

The transition is phased and region-specific. We do not shut off the lights to win an argument.

  • Phase the mix: diversify generation and retire the dirtiest sources first.
  • Keep reliability: maintain firm capacity and grid services during transitions.
  • Electrify smart: building efficiency, heat pumps, and industrial upgrades reduce load growth pressure.
  • End waste: efficiency is the cheapest energy.
Rural opportunity

Energy as local income

  • Community-owned generation: solar, wind, and storage with local revenue sharing.
  • Right-of-way fairness: landowners and communities receive fair compensation and transparency.
  • Grid access: prevent monopolies from blocking interconnection for local projects.
  • Workforce: paid training pipelines and local hiring standards.
Outcome: rural communities gain stable revenue and jobs without being exploited.
Energy independence

Reduce foreign leverage

  • Domestic supply chains: invest in manufacturing and critical minerals processing with high standards.
  • Strategic reserves: manage fuel reserves to reduce shock exposure.
  • Demand reduction: efficiency and transit reduce dependence on volatile fuels.

Independence is achieved through diversified supply and lower vulnerability—not slogans.

Implementation

First-year build sequence

  1. Publish energy dashboards: rates, outages, restoration times, and utility profits.
  2. Launch grid modernization via public banking and infrastructure corps capacity.
  3. Set reliability + affordability rules with enforceable benchmarks.
  4. Scale local generation with community ownership models and fair interconnection.
  5. Phase transition based on regional needs while protecting workers and ratepayers.
