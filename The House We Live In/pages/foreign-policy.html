Principle

Non-imperial, non-isolationist: a working-majority foreign policy

The United States cannot build prosperity at home while running permanent conflict abroad or writing trade rules that hollow out communities. A working-majority republic pursues a foreign policy of stability, dignity, alliances, and fair trade—without domination and without retreat.

Goal: improve America’s position in the world by strengthening our legitimacy at home and credibility abroad.
Core commitments

What never changes

  • Defense of the homeland and allies remains a core duty.
  • No regime-change-for-profit foreign policy.
  • Trade must meet labor and environmental standards so workers are not sacrificed.
  • Diplomacy first with clear red lines and enforceable treaties.
  • Human rights with consistency: no selective outrage based on profit.
Trade and the working class

Trade agreements must benefit workers

  • Labor standards: enforceable wages, safety, and anti-child-labor requirements.
  • Anti-offshoring rules: stop tax-haven and labor-arbitrage games.
  • Domestic capacity: strategic industries and supply chains maintained at home.
  • Reciprocity: market access depends on fair play, not exploitation.
Outcome: globalization becomes a two-way street instead of a one-way extraction pipe.
Global competitiveness

How we stay ahead without domination

  • Innovation leadership: public R&D, open standards, and trustworthy AI become export advantages.
  • Energy resilience: stable domestic energy reduces geopolitical vulnerability.
  • Infrastructure leadership: build at home; partner abroad without debt traps.
  • Anti-corruption diplomacy: target kleptocracy and money laundering networks.
Alliances and diplomacy

Credibility through predictability

  • Strengthen alliances: shared defense plus shared economic rules that protect workers.
  • Predictable commitments: reduce whiplash by aligning foreign policy with public interests.
  • Conflict prevention: invest in diplomacy and early warning instead of escalation.
War powers and democratic control

No blank checks for endless war

  • War powers discipline: time-limited authorizations, mandatory renewals, and public reporting.
  • Contract transparency: defense contracting and reconstruction spending fully auditable.
  • Veteran protection: service members receive lifetime healthcare and support (tied to the defense pillar).
Bright line: foreign policy cannot be a profit center for a small class of contractors.
Implementation

First-year build sequence

  1. Publish a Working-Majority Foreign Policy Doctrine centered on stability, alliances, and fair trade.
  2. Rewrite trade rules with labor/environment enforcement and anti-haven provisions.
  3. Anti-corruption enforcement: money laundering, shell networks, and foreign influence transparency.
  4. War powers reform: time limits, renewals, and contract audits.
  5. Build credibility through predictable diplomacy and domestic legitimacy.
