Mechanics — Quality of Life Index (v1)

Last updated: 2026-02-01. Act III anchor: Room 072 (Beam).

Purpose

Provide a single, inspectable score (0–100) that summarizes what Act III claims: quality of life rises when cost pressure falls, time returns, stability increases, and fear decreases.

This is a v1 scaffold: simple on purpose, upgradeable when measured data is wired.

Inputs

  • cost_strain_pct — percent of income under strain (10–70 in v1 UI)
  • time_recovered_hours_per_week — hours regained (0–20 in v1 UI)
  • stability_score — 0–100
  • fear_score — 0–100 (higher means more fear/uncertainty)

Normalization (explicit choices)

Inputs are converted to component scores (0–100). These curves are simple placeholders—chosen for legibility, not as claims of empirical truth.

  • Cost relief score: 10% strain → 100, 70% strain → 0 (linear)
  • Time regained score: 0h/wk → 0, 20h/wk → 100 (linear)
  • Stability score: passed through as-is
  • Fear reduction score: 0 fear → 100, 100 fear → 0 (inverse)

Upgrade path: replace these with empirically calibrated curves once baseline distributions exist.

Formula

QOL is a weighted sum of component scores:

QOL = 0.35·CostRelief + 0.25·TimeRegained + 0.25·Stability + 0.15·FearReduction

The weights are fixed in v1 to keep comparisons consistent across pages.

Uncertainty (v1 honesty band)

The tool shows a simple band by shifting the cost weight by ±5 points and redistributing the remainder proportionally to other components. This is not statistical error. It is a transparency placeholder.

Upgrade path: empirical uncertainty using measurement error + scenario variance.

Assumptions

  • Act III B5: quality of life is measurable enough to track meaningfully.
  • Normalization choice: linear curves are acceptable as a v1 placeholder.
  • Weight choice: the weights reflect Act III’s narrative emphasis (cost/time/stability/fear).
