Transparency standard

How we keep this honest

  • Assumption: what we’re assuming (and why)
  • Mechanism: how the policy changes the metric
  • Directionality: why it should improve/worsen
  • Range: low/base/high to avoid fake precision
  • Rationale: plain language logic and (later) citations
Rule: every number must be explainable to a skeptical reader.
Index structure

Domains and weights

QoLI is a composite of nine domains. Initial version uses equal weights for defensibility. Later versions add user-adjustable weights and household presets (rural, single-parent, retired, etc.).

  • Income & Work Security
  • Cost of Living & Affordability
  • Housing Stability & Ownership Path
  • Health & Longevity
  • Education & Mobility
  • Safety, Justice & Community Trust
  • Time & Stress
  • Infrastructure Access
  • Democratic Agency
True costs

Household + system costs

We publish both:

  • Household costs: premiums/out-of-pocket, rent/mortgage, utilities, transport, debt payments, time burden.
  • System costs: public spending, private spending, hidden costs (bankruptcy, ER overuse, crime/incarceration, disasters, crises).

The status quo often hides costs by pushing them onto families. This model surfaces them.

Data format

How the site stores and updates the model

  • /data/qoli/config.json — domains, weights, timepoints
  • /data/qoli/scenarios.json — scenario definitions and ranges
  • /data/qoli/households.json — baseline households
  • /data/qoli/domains/*.json — indicator definitions per domain
  • /data/qoli/outputs/*.json — computed outputs displayed on the site
A script generates outputs so the site stays static and fast, with full transparency.
