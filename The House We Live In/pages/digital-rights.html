Personal sovereignty

Personal data ownership and freedom from coercion

  • Personal data belongs to the individual by default; organizations receive time-limited, revocable licenses.
  • At any time, individuals can request a complete copy of all personal and derived data held about them.
  • It is illegal to discriminate against anyone for withholding or negotiating data use.
  • Collective negotiation is supported: people can delegate bargaining to trusted civic/consumer groups.
Bright line: surveillance of private people in private spaces is illegal without individualized warrants and strict limits.
Philosophy

Privacy is the new civil rights frontier

If participation in society requires surrendering control of personal information, then freedom becomes conditional and democracy becomes performative. Data extraction creates behavioral control: pricing, hiring, housing, politics, and speech become steerable at scale.

Digital civil rights restores personhood: you own your data; you can refuse; you cannot be punished for refusing.

Non-negotiable right

Freedom from data coercion and retaliation

  • It is illegal to discriminate against anyone for withholding, limiting, or negotiating data use.
  • No “privacy penalty tiers.” No worse pricing. No reduced service. No retaliation.
  • Only strictly necessary data can be required to deliver a service. Everything else is optional and negotiable.
Bright line: ambiguous data contracts are interpreted against the collector. Hidden clauses are unenforceable.
Collective bargaining for data

Negotiation “by the whole”

Individuals should not have to negotiate with trillion‑dollar platforms alone. People can delegate bargaining rights to trusted groups—consumer unions, civic trusts, or worker organizations—who negotiate standard data terms on behalf of members.

  • Standardized plain-language contracts
  • Transparent pricing/value of data use
  • Revocable licenses with clear expiration
Surveillance limits

Private spaces are inviolable

  • Surveillance of private people in private spaces is illegal without individualized warrants and strict limits.
  • Public observation is allowed, but persistent identity tracking and biometric mass surveillance are restricted and auditable.
  • High-stakes algorithmic decisions require explanation, appeal, and human review.
