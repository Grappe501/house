Civil Rights & Religious Neutrality

Home / The Model / Civil Rights
Principle

Equal civil rights without religious warfare

The system protects religious freedom and equal civil rights by drawing a clear line: people are free to believe, worship, preach, and organize—but government may not privilege a faith or impose doctrine.

Core idea: freedom of religion requires freedom from religious control by the state.
How we avoid polarization

Frame it as liberty for everyone

  • Neutrality: government does not pick winners among religions or non-religion.
  • Equal dignity: civil rights are not negotiated by theology.
  • Freedom of conscience: individuals can live their faith without using state power to control others.

This is not anti-faith. It is anti-theocracy and anti-coercion.

Separation of church and state

Enforcement mechanisms

  • Public money neutrality: no public funding for religious indoctrination; if public funds touch an institution, they cannot fund sectarian instruction.
  • Public office neutrality: officials cannot use state authority to impose religious tests or doctrine.
  • Policy basis rule: laws must be justified in public reasons that apply to all citizens, not sectarian claims.
  • Civil rights enforcement: independent civil rights commission with subpoena power and local enforcement partnerships.
Bright line: your right to belief ends at coercing another person’s rights.
Local governance compatibility

Decentralization with rights floors

Decentralized governance only works if constitutional rights floors are real. Local communities govern daily life, but they cannot violate equal protection, due process, voting rights, or basic freedoms.
