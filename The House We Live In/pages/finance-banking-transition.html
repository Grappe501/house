Finance & Banking Transition

Home / The Model / Finance
Philosophy

Finance must serve the productive economy

Wall Street is not “bad” because markets exist. It becomes destructive when it extracts wealth from workers and local communities without building real capacity. A working-majority republic keeps capitalism—but rewires incentives so finance earns returns by funding production, infrastructure, and innovation.

Goal: make the system better for honest capital while ending extraction, monopoly, and crisis cycles.
The core problem

Extraction + monopoly + crisis

  • Extraction: fees, buybacks, rent seeking, predatory lending, and financialization of housing/health.
  • Monopoly: concentrated corporate control and single-vendor dominance across sectors.
  • Crisis cycles: privatized gains, socialized losses; working families absorb volatility.

The goal is not to “destroy markets.” It is to destroy the incentives that reward harm.

Public banking rails

A parallel system that stabilizes everything

  • National public bank framework with locally governed public banks and credit unions as primary partners.
  • Infrastructure finance: long-term, low-cost capital for water, grid, broadband, transport.
  • Small business credit: transparent underwriting focused on community value and job creation.
  • Housing rails: low-fee mortgages, automatic refinancing, anti-foreclosure mediation.
Design: build a “safe lane” that pulls the economy away from speculative dependence over time.
Investor class transition

Make it better for them early, then level out

To avoid collapse and political warfare, the transition offers investors a superior long-term deal:

  • Stable returns: infrastructure and productivity-linked bonds with predictable yields.
  • Reduced volatility: fewer boom-bust cycles increases long-run compounding.
  • Productive opportunities: channel capital into manufacturing, energy, housing supply, and local enterprise.
  • Clear rules: eliminate regulatory whiplash by codifying anti-extraction standards.

Speculation becomes less profitable; productive investment becomes more profitable.

Breaking monopoly power

Bust up the five-firm choke points

  • Antitrust modernization: treat platform dominance, vertical integration abuse, and algorithmic collusion as major threats.
  • Interoperability rules: prevent lock-in and create competitive entry points.
  • Ban predatory acquisitions: stop “buy-or-kill” strategies in tech and healthcare.
  • Procurement diversity: federal and state procurement breaks vendor concentration.
Bright line: no single company should be able to control a nation’s basic needs or information flows.
Billionaires & wealth limits

What happens when we cap income/wealth

  • Hard caps are phased: to prevent sudden flight and allow markets to adapt.
  • Ownership diversification: large holdings shift gradually into public-benefit trusts and broad citizen ownership rails.
  • Philanthropy rules: philanthropy cannot substitute for democracy; it must be transparent and non-coercive.
  • Exit taxes and anti-haven rules: prevent offshore games and tax-base flight.

The goal is to end oligarchy—not entrepreneurship.

Growing GDP while sharing wealth locally

How we expand capacity

  • Health + education increases human capital and labor participation.
  • Infrastructure buildout reduces transaction costs and raises productivity.
  • Local reinvestment keeps money circulating, multiplying regional output.
  • Small business formation increases competition and innovation.
Core engine: less extraction + more capacity = higher GDP with broader distribution.
Implementation

A pragmatic transition sequence

  1. Stand up public banking rails for housing, small business, and infrastructure.
  2. Shift incentives via tax and regulatory reform: reward productive investment over speculation.
  3. Antitrust wave to break choke points and open markets.
  4. Wealth dividend expansion through People’s Wealth Fund as productivity grows.
  5. Phase in wealth limits with anti-haven enforcement and transparent ownership rules.
