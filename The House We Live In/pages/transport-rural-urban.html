Why this matters

Distance should not equal disadvantage

Rural life often means longer drives for healthcare, groceries, school events, work, and civic participation. The result is higher household transportation burden and fewer opportunities—especially for young people and seniors.

This page explains the rural-to-urban mobility design in detail.

The rural mobility package

What every region gets

  • Mobility hubs: safe parking, charging, secure bikes, weather shelter, restrooms, and information.
  • Feeder service: demand-responsive shuttles that connect outlying homes to hubs.
  • Express service: scheduled express coaches (and rail where feasible) to regional centers.
  • Service guarantees: “last trip home” protection so riders aren’t stranded.
Projecting a real future

What changes within 5–10 years

  • Every county has at least one true mobility hub with timed transfers.
  • Express corridor service connects rural hubs to job/health/education centers multiple times daily.
  • Older adults and teenagers gain mobility without requiring a household vehicle for every driver.
  • Rural housing demand increases as commute burden falls, easing pressure on urban cores.
