Student Debt Reset & Education Debt Abolition

Home / The Model / Student Debt
Principle

Education debt is an extraction system

When a society requires education for dignity and mobility, it cannot fund that education by chaining working people to decades of compounding debt. Student debt is not only personal—it is a drag on births, homeownership, entrepreneurship, rural revival, and long-run GDP.

Goal: abolish legacy student debt and make education free going forward.
What happens to past student debt

A one-time debt reset

  • Federal student loans: abolished (principal and interest) through statutory mechanisms and Treasury/balance-sheet conversion.
  • Private student loans: converted through a regulated buyout/refinance program that prevents windfall profiteering.
  • No tax bomb: forgiveness is not treated as taxable income.
  • Credit protection: credit reporting repaired automatically—no lasting penalty for being trapped.
Bright line: we end the debt without creating a new extraction pipeline for lenders.
Why it’s fair

We all benefit from educated people

  • Educated workers raise productivity and civic capacity.
  • Teachers, nurses, engineers, and trades strengthen every community.
  • Debt abolition functions like infrastructure—an investment that pays back through growth and stability.
How we avoid “moral hazard”

Fix the upstream system

  • Free public education (already defined): tuition-free community college, universities, trade programs.
  • Price discipline: public cost controls and transparency for programs receiving public support.
  • Living stipends for need: targeted supports for housing/food/transport so students can complete.
  • Workforce alignment: apprenticeships and paid pathways reduce debt reliance.
Transition mechanics

Do it without crashing markets

  • Phase 1: stop interest accrual and collections immediately.
  • Phase 2: cancel federal balances; convert servicing to a public administration wind-down.
  • Phase 3: private loan conversion via capped buyout prices + refinance rails through public banking.
  • Phase 4: publish transparency on institutions and predatory programs; create restitution mechanisms where fraud occurred.

Markets absorb policy shifts best when the state acts clearly, quickly, and with defined rules.

Local and national outcomes

What changes in real life

  • Homeownership rises: down payments become possible; debt-to-income improves.
  • Business formation rises: fewer people delay risk-taking due to debt fear.
  • Family stability improves: reduced stress, better mobility, earlier family formation for those who want it.
  • Rural revival strengthens: graduates can return home without debt pressure forcing metro migration.
